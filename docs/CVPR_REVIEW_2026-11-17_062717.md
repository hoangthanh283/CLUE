## Reviewer summary

The paper investigates replay failure in partially annotated continual key-information extraction and proposes CoLaR-SBR, combining compressed document latents with session-specific background readouts. The most convincing result is not the large gap over weak ports: it is the fixed-memory comparison and the different responses to supervision repairs across CORD, FUNSD and WildReceipt. Relabelling stored documents nearly rescues CORD but fails on FUNSD and is unstable on WildReceipt. A bidirectional MiB-style loss rescues FUNSD but leaves substantial gaps on the other two. SBR performs well on all three.

This is a worthwhile empirical contribution. However, background shift, partial-label losses, separated softmaxes, latent replay and per-session isolation are established ingredients. The paper must earn its significance through reliable controls, a precise explanation of what is new on documents, and evidence about when its proposed combination is needed. Successful interventions do not uniquely identify drift versus conflict, and the strongest remaining competitor question is not answered by many failing ports.

## Strengths

1. **Large matched-memory effect.** CoLaR to CoLaR-SBR changes the readout while retaining the latent-memory recipe. Full-label F1 avoids the coverage limitation of session-restricted AA. The CORD, FUNSD and WildReceipt effects are large and positive on all three seeds.
2. **Informative counterexamples.** FUNSD contradicts a universal stored-label explanation. WildReceipt now adds another distinct response rather than an empty column. This is more informative than suppressing failed repairs or claiming one universal mechanism.
3. **Strong CORD controls.** The pre-session relabelling oracle, mask-only oracle, current/replay slicing, raw memory with SBR, constant-O SBR and no-memory SBR distinguish several alternatives. The mask-only oracle is particularly useful because it removes false negatives without supplying new positive labels.
4. **Honest costs and limits.** CoLaR is not smaller than 8-bit raw storage; latent features can leak content; Recall's parameters and inference passes grow with sessions. Language shift and image results limit the scope. The author’s method-led framing is preserved, with narrower causal language.
5. **Inspectable provenance.** Tables and figures can be regenerated from saved files, and the new paired bootstrap verifies both token alignment and pooled entity-span scoring. This is stronger than relying on remembered aggregate numbers.

## Main weaknesses

**W1 — Closest-baseline fidelity still limits the novelty claim.** The supplement says the ports have not been checked against published NER results. Several published structures were replaced with a common growing token head. The one-seed FUNSD patience probe addresses one optimisation choice, not implementation fidelity, learning-rate selection, or the adequacy of CORD adaptation. ICE-O with a frozen encoder is not a matched adaptive-trunk competitor. The constant-O readout with the same memory is the closer control: on CORD it scores 75.3 / 82.8 / 89.2, versus SBR 88.8 / 91.2 / 87.1. A learned background is therefore not uniformly better. The method’s value must include the diagnostic study and the combination, not invention or universal necessity of a learned background.

**W2 — The new slicing control was overstated.** `configs/method/colar_r_sliced2.yaml` sets `current_sliced: true`. In `doccl/methods/latent_replay.py::_current_ce`, this removes old logits only for current tokens labelled O; positive current tokens retain the full softmax. Replay in `doccl/methods/colar_r.py::sliced_replay_loss` retains all classes known at capture time. This is a useful partial-label control, but it is not a faithful SS-IL or ER-ACE reproduction. I corrected the equivalence claim and the inference that separate backgrounds are necessary. The alternative `current_sliced: all` exists in code, but its existence is not evidence of a completed matched experiment.

**W3 — Repair efficacy is not unique causal identification.** The oracle changes positive labels as well as false negatives; MiB changes both current and replay losses; SBR changes gradients, normalisation and routing. The 2×2 interaction does not isolate a unique two-factor mechanism. SBR also helps on ImageNet-R without an O class, and the CORD shared-softmax oracle succeeds without SBR. These observations rule out a universal necessity reading. The exact reason for the stream-dependent repairs remains open.

**W4 — Clean reruns do not erase exploratory selection.** CORD's headline comparison is now clean, a material improvement. However, Mixed DER++ and Mixed Recall retain legacy test-selected cells; WildReceipt DER++ still has only two clean seeds in this snapshot. More broadly, the comparison set and some choices followed exploratory results on these scenarios. Held-out early stopping in the final reruns does not make that earlier model/method selection independent of test outcomes. The updated table should not be described as wholly prospective or wholly clean.

**W5 — Order and deployment scope remain limited.** The full ladder uses one CORD order. At the inspected snapshot, order 2 has CoLaR and Recall at seed 42, and order 3 has CoLaR; the complete matched method triplets and multi-seed order checks are not available. The overlapped partial-label protocol is stated clearly, but reappearing receipts under synthetic masks are not equivalent to a disjoint deployment stream. Recall's routing and initial base could be order-sensitive. Three training seeds are not a calibrated uncertainty model for new document domains.

**W6 — Presentation and uncertainty.** Table 4 is very dense and uses full-label F1 for CORD but AA for the other CIL columns; Table 2 is the more legible cross-stream full-label comparison. Small differences described as “level with” joint are descriptive, not equivalence tests. The new bootstrap conditions on one fitted seed-42 model pair; it does not establish multi-seed significance, template independence, or transport to another dataset.

## Angular Steering: what the comparison does and does not imply

The [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26243) presents activation steering through a selected two-dimensional plane, relates earlier steering operators geometrically, and evaluates behavior control alongside capability preservation. Its appendix includes emotion experiments and explicit algorithms. That is a different contribution from a memory/readout composition for continual KIE. Its relevance is the standard of explanation and testing, not a direct baseline or an acceptance precedent.
