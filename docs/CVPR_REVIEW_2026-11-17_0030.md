## Reviewer assessment

**Subjective score: 5.5/10, borderline reject; reasonable reviewer range 5–6.** This is my explicit ten-point assessment, not an official CVPR scale or an acceptance probability. Claim corrections improve credibility but do not supply missing experiments. I would not move the paper above 6 solely for prose polishing.

The paper studies a useful failure: replay retains partially annotated documents whose background labels conflict with expanding entity classes. The strongest contribution is the intervention evidence and its stream dependence, not invention of separated softmaxes. CORD's stored-label oracle rescues performance, FUNSD's does not, and a bidirectional background-aware loss rescues FUNSD. That asymmetry is more interesting than another broad table of weakly performing ports.

Strengths:

- Large, reproducible improvements under a fixed memory and encoder; full-label evaluation avoids the known coverage limitation of session-restricted AA.
- The CORD mask-only oracle is an especially valuable control: removal of false negatives rescues performance without adding positive labels. It strengthens the interpretation beyond relabelling alone.
- FUNSD is a real boundary case rather than a suppressed negative. Its existing current-only and bidirectional slicing controls must be credited, not requested again as missing.
- Raw replay plus SBR, constant-background SBR, no-memory SBR, BROS, and memory-byte accounting substantially narrow the claims. CoLaR is not claimed to beat raw 8-bit storage in bytes.
- Failure on language shift and the cost of per-session deltas are disclosed.

Major weaknesses:

1. **Closest-baseline credibility.** ICE-O/MORE/SS-IL and ER-ACE are conceptually close. A frozen ICE-O port is not a matched adaptive-trunk competitor. Constant-O with the same memory is more informative and should anchor the novelty discussion. CPFD/IS3 and other ports often fail to learn; the supplement explicitly says they are not checked against published NER results. A one-seed FUNSD patience probe cannot validate their implementations or establish that their CORD failures are intrinsic.
2. **Protocol mixture and selection history.** Table 4 mixes clean full-label F1, clean AA and daggered test-selected AA. LCA even has a clean one-seed F1 next to legacy AA. The generator keeps protocols separate within a cell, but adjacent cells do not necessarily describe the same runs. Reproducing the table is not proof that baseline selection/tuning was unbiased. A comparison set fixed after observing an initial grid remains exploratory.
3. **Mechanism versus successful repair.** Relabelling changes positive supervision as well as false negatives; bidirectional MiB changes both current and replay losses; SBR changes loss geometry and routing. Superadditivity in the 2x2 is not unique identification of two causes. Generic separated-softmax gains on images are an alternative contributor. The revised prose now states these boundaries more clearly.
4. **Incomplete scope tests.** WildReceipt oracle and MiB results are absent at this snapshot. No completed CORD o2/o3 metrics were found locally or in the remote snapshot. CORD current-only and bidirectional slicing each have only two completed seeds. A single default class order cannot support order robustness, especially for Recall.
5. **External validity and significance.** Overlapped documents under synthetic partial masks are a defensible protocol, but not equivalent to a genuinely disjoint incoming document stream. Three training seeds and small document test sets establish consistent signs, not calibrated uncertainty over documents or deployment shifts. Background-logit gains are not uniformly positive on LayoutLMv3.

## Angular Steering comparison

Sources: [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26243), [public OpenReview discussion](https://openreview.net/forum?id=dGi2d5yDs4). The live OpenReview endpoint returned a browser challenge; the supplied JSON-string capture was decoded and used for the reviews. Its historical authenticity was not independently revalidated. The paper PDF was accessed directly.

Angular Steering presents rotation-based activation control and a geometric connection to earlier steering operations. Its relevance here is how a simple mechanism earns significance through a unifying explanation, close comparisons and boundary tests—not that its technique is a relevant KIE baseline.

The saved reviews show XDYP, D7pZ and pfta at **5: Accept**, and cNXF at **4: Borderline accept**. These are categorical recommendations on that venue's scale, **not 5/10 or 4/10**, and should not be numerically converted to my score. The captured decision is Accept (spotlight).

XDYP wanted behavior breadth and small-model robustness; D7pZ wanted a principled plane choice and complexity explanation; cNXF questioned novelty, behavior breadth and unsupported preservation claims; pfta requested direct steering comparisons. The decision credits the rebuttal's added emotion experiments and direct comparisons. A residual novelty concern remains in cNXF's final justification despite the positive decision.

For this paper, the corresponding lesson is to make the known readout's incremental contribution precise, validate the closest controls, and test the proposed explanation where it might fail. Six named streams and many poor ports do not substitute for those tests. The favorable outcome of a different paper does not predict this paper's acceptance.

## Evidence needed for a defensible ≥6

Ordered by likely value, not a new authorized training queue:

1. **Validate the closest baselines and clean comparisons.** Use completed clean baseline seeds when the owners finish them; expose source/protocol per metric. Demonstrate that at least one strong partial-label / separated-readout comparator is faithfully implemented and learns under a reasonable validation-selected recipe. Compare matched memory, frozen layers and adaptation budgets. Constant-O SBR and bidirectional MiB already supply useful anchors.
2. **Finish the discriminating scope tests.** Inspect the owner's WildReceipt oracle/MiB outputs and class-order runs. Report all seeds, including failures. If unavailable by freeze, retain the narrowed empirical claim and explicitly mark the missing contrasts. Merely filling a third dataset column does not by itself prove causality.
3. **Explain the CORD/FUNSD difference using existing predictions and controlled contrasts.** Connect current-only versus bidirectional slicing, mask-only versus positive relabelling, per-session learning and error types. A post-hoc explanation must remain labelled as such until tested.
4. **Make the novelty case about the diagnosis.** Clearly separate inherited readout design from new document evidence. Avoid claiming that joint normalization is necessarily wrong whenever valid negatives are restricted; the successful shared-softmax oracle is a counterexample to a universal necessity claim.
5. **Strengthen uncertainty and usability.** Where saved document-level predictions permit, use paired document resampling; do not infer document uncertainty from three training seeds. Improve the very dense main comparison and supplement tables if space permits. Keep full-label F1 prominent for CIL.

These are review conditions, not an acceptance guarantee or a requirement to run every named background-shift method.
