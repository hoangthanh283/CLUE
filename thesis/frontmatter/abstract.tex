\abstractpage{
Deployed document-understanding systems must absorb new document types, schemas, and
domains over time without forgetting what they already know. Multimodal document encoders
such as LayoutLMv3 fuse text, layout, and visual pathways in one heterogeneous network, yet
the continual-learning remedies applied to them treat the network as a homogeneous block
and were developed on unimodal classification. This thesis first asks \emph{where} such an
encoder forgets. A controlled per-component diagnosis---input-modality masks on a single
LayoutLMv3 architecture, read out through per-token representational drift (linear CKA),
old-task-Fisher-weighted parameter displacement, and standard forgetting metrics over the
FUNSD\,$\rightarrow$\,CORD\,$\rightarrow$\,SROIE sequence---returns a clear result: forgetting is
\emph{output-side}, concentrated in the classifier head and the late encoder layers (CKA
falls monotonically with depth from $1.00$ at the input encoders to $0.16$ at the head), it is
not fusion-specific, and it is architecture-general: LiLT, BROS, and a text-only BERT
reproduce the same locus. Protecting that locus does not remove the forgetting but relocates
it to whatever remains plastic, and a root-cause analysis identifies the mechanism as a
\emph{readout-marginal snap}: the head's output distribution collapses onto the newest task's
label marginal, extinguishing never-re-exercised classes.

The diagnosis dictates what a remedy must do: re-exercise the head with old-task gradients
that arrive through whole documents, because the (feature, position, label) binding of a page
is the unit of competence. We confirm this constructively and by falsification. Across six
classical strategies, four backbones, three scenarios, and three seeds, only replay re-grounds
the head---ER and DER++ reach the joint-training upper bound on domain-incremental learning
(average entity-F1 $\approx 88$ vs.\ $88.7$)---while five buffer-free families built or ported
for this work (weight merging, subspace transfer, parametric slot memories, input-anchored
memory, and feature-Gaussian replay) fail for that single identified reason, and five
pre-registered recalibration kill-tests recover nothing. Every marginal summary of a document
collapses to the naive level, whereas per-document rank-$r$ compression of stored latent
activations (\textbf{CoLaR}) is lossless at $2.7\times$ ($87.6$ AA at $60$\,MB).

We then build the proposed method, \textbf{LexSlot}, on exactly these two findings. The
latent store is the memory; the drift-immune lexical signature of a document---a bag of its OCR
tokens, which identifies the source task at $0.92$ across document families---is the
\emph{router} that decides which stored documents are replayed against each incoming batch, so
that replay is targeted at the documents most likely to interfere rather than drawn uniformly.
Routing is applied on the replay side only, because inference-time routing over stored latents
is shown here to be inert or destructive. \todo{On domain-incremental learning LexSlot reaches
AA $X$ at $50$ documents per task and $Y$ at $5$ documents per task, against $86.4$ and $66.5$ for
uniformly sampled latent replay at equal memory, over three seeds.} A pre-registered
image-classification scope test (ViT-B/16 on Split CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-R) shows that the
anatomy transfers but the remedy ordering does not: with a single class-level head, a buffer-free
re-grounding of the head suffices, which locates the boundary of the document result in the
per-token head rather than in the modality. These results establish the per-component
diagnostic protocol, the falsification chain, and a diagnosis-guided, memory-efficient replay
method as practical instruments for continual document understanding.\footnote{Source code:
\url{https://github.com/hoangthanh283/doccl} (\texttt{doccl} branch); experiments tracked on
Weights \& Biases at \url{https://wandb.ai/thanh-workspace/CL4IE}.}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} continual learning, catastrophic forgetting, document
understanding, key-information extraction, LayoutLMv3, latent replay, lexical routing.
}
