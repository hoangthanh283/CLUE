\abstractpage{
Deployed document-understanding systems must absorb new document types, schemas, and
domains over time without forgetting what they already know, and they usually cannot keep
the raw documents they were trained on. Multimodal document encoders such as LayoutLMv3
meet the demand for multimodal understanding by fusing text, layout, and visual pathways
into a single network, yet it remains unknown which part of this heterogeneous network
drives catastrophic forgetting. Prevailing continual-learning methods do not ask: they
regularise the network uniformly or freeze layers by hand-tuned heuristics, and even
parameter-efficient and modular adaptation presupposes knowing where to intervene. The
document setting adds two difficulties of its own, since key-information extraction is dense
token-level sequence labelling rather than the classification most continual-learning
methods were built for, and document features are close to full rank, so past tasks cannot
be compressed into a small summary.

This thesis studies a stream of key-information-extraction tasks, in which scanned documents
arrive as page images, OCR tokens, and bounding boxes with BIO entity labels, under
class-incremental, domain-incremental, and mixed regimes. A pre-trained tri-modal encoder
learns them in sequence with a bounded memory and no access to past raw documents, and must
end as a single model that extracts entities from every task seen so far. We first locate
where in the encoder forgetting concentrates, then use that answer to decide what a
memory-bounded remedy must retain and which of its stored evidence it should re-expose,
arriving at a single method, \textbf{lexically-routed latent replay}, in which each of these
choices follows from a measurement.

The diagnosis masks the input modalities of a single LayoutLMv3, holding the architecture
fixed, and reads forgetting out through linear CKA drift, per-group Fisher information, and
Fisher-weighted parameter displacement at every task boundary, with LiLT, BROS, and a
text-only BERT as contrasts. It shows that forgetting is output-side: the classifier head
has over ten times the Fisher importance of any other parameter group, and CKA
drift rises monotonically with depth, from $1.00$ at the input encoders to $0.25$ at the late
layers and $0.16$ at the head. The same site recurs on every backbone, including the unimodal
one, so it is not a multimodal artefact. Protecting it does not help, however: freezing the
head and late layers merely moves the drift to whatever remains plastic, which suggests that a
remedy should re-supply old-task evidence at this site rather than protect its parameters.

A ten-method comparison over three seeds bears this out. Regularisation, distillation, and
prompt- or LoRA-based methods fail for a common reason, as the head's output marginal snaps
to the newest task while the representation beneath it drifts, and pre-registered eval-time
and marginal-level corrections recover nothing. Replay alone closes the gap, reaching an
average entity-F1 of about $88$ on domain-incremental learning against the joint oracle's
$88.7$, but only when it replays whole documents: every summary that breaks the binding of
feature, position, and label collapses to the naive level, even when built from real
activations. Individual documents, unlike the pooled feature space, are nevertheless low-rank,
so storing each past document as its own low-rank factors at an intermediate layer (CoLaR)
preserves that binding and reaches $87.6$ with a $60$\,MB memory, $2.7\times$ smaller than raw
replay and without keeping any raw page.

The proposed method builds on this memory and changes only how it is read. Rather than
sampling stored documents uniformly, it routes replay by an OCR-vocabulary signature that
depends on the input alone and therefore cannot drift, the same signal that gates LexSlot's
slot memory at the diagnosed site, so that each incoming batch is rehearsed against the stored
documents most relevant to it. Together with the diagnostic protocol, the benchmark, and the
tooling released with the thesis, the method offers a measurement-driven route to continual
learning for multimodal document encoders.\footnote{Source code:
\url{https://github.com/hoangthanh283/doccl} (\texttt{doccl} branch); experiments tracked on
Weights \& Biases at \url{https://wandb.ai/thanh-workspace/doccl-aaai2027}.}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} continual learning, catastrophic forgetting, document
understanding, key-information extraction, LayoutLMv3, latent replay.
}
