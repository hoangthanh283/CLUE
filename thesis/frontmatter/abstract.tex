\abstractpage{
Deployed document-understanding systems must absorb new document types, schemas,
and domains over time without forgetting what they already know. Multimodal
document encoders such as LayoutLMv3 meet this demand by fusing text, layout, and
visual pathways into a single network, yet that network is architecturally
heterogeneous and it remains unknown \emph{which} pathway drives catastrophic
forgetting. This gap matters because prevailing continual-learning methods are
blind to such structure---they regularise the network uniformly or freeze layers
by hand-tuned heuristics---while modern parameter-efficient and modular adaptation
implicitly presupposes knowing where to intervene. This thesis closes the gap with
a per-layer, per-parameter-group diagnosis of how forgetting distributes across the
text, visual, layout, and cross-modal fusion components of a tri-modal document
encoder under continual sequence-labelling for key-information extraction. A
controlled, apples-to-apples protocol masks individual input modalities of a single
LayoutLMv3 architecture---isolating each component's contribution without the
confound of comparing different models---and reads forgetting out through
representational drift (linear CKA), per-group Fisher information, and standard
forgetting metrics across the FUNSD\,$\rightarrow$\,CORD\,$\rightarrow$\,SROIE task
sequence. The diagnosis returns a clear, somewhat counter-intuitive result: forgetting
is \emph{not} fusion-specific but \emph{output-side}---it concentrates in the
classifier head and the late encoder layers (the head carries several times more
Fisher importance than any other group and CKA drift rises monotonically with depth, from
$1.00$ at the input encoders to $0.22$ at the head), while the input encoders remain
comparatively stable; a controlled cross-condition test cannot distinguish the full
multimodal model's forgetting from its unimodal ablations.

We then benchmark six continual-learning strategies---naive and joint bounds,
regularisation (EWC), distillation (LwF), and replay (ER, DER++)---across three
scenarios (class-, domain-, and mixed-incremental) over three seeds. The empirical
ordering follows directly from the diagnosis: because forgetting is at the head,
\emph{replay}---which re-exposes old-class targets at the output---is the strongest
remedy, with ER and DER++ reaching the joint upper bound on domain-incremental
learning (average entity-F1 $\approx 88$ versus the oracle's $88.7$, with backward
transfer near zero) and closing essentially the entire forgetting gap; regularisation
(EWC) roughly halves forgetting where a domain shift is present, and distillation (LwF)
gives little benefit in this dense token-classification setting. Guided by the same
measurement we design \emph{two} mechanism-targeted remedies that concentrate
continual-learning effort on the dominant-forgetting head and late layers. The first,
\textbf{DocCL}, combines depth-scaled consolidation, output distillation, and head replay.
Measured over three
seeds, DocCL \emph{closes most of} the domain-incremental forgetting gap (average
entity-F1 $84.7$ versus the oracle's $88.7$, within $\sim$4 points, backward transfer
near zero) but does \emph{not} reach it, and does not surpass experience replay,
which remains $\sim$3 points higher; on the mixed and class-incremental scenarios DocCL
tracks the strong baselines without exceeding them. The second, \textbf{LexSlot},
protects the same locus with a parameter-efficient, isolated slot memory gated by a
drift-immune lexical signature and \emph{needs no replay buffer}; on the domain-incremental
scenario it reaches replay-level accuracy ($88.4$) with the least forgetting of any
non-oracle method ($-1.1$ backward transfer), though this result is single-seed on one
scenario and backbone so far, pending the full grid. A depth-targeting ablation confirms
the diagnosis: penalising every depth \emph{uniformly}---rather than concentrating the
budget on the diagnosed head/late locus---collapses ($42.6$ AA) even though it spends a
larger total budget, whereas concentrating it on that locus recovers the full result. The honest finding is therefore that diagnosis-driven head/late targeting closes
most of the forgetting gap on a domain shift, performing competitively with---though not
beating---data replay. These
results establish the per-component diagnostic protocol, together with a controlled
benchmark and per-component tooling that we release, as a practical instrument for
designing continual-learning methods for multimodal document
encoders.\footnote{Source code: \url{https://github.com/hoangthanh283/doccl}
(\texttt{doccl} branch); experiments tracked on Weights \& Biases at
\url{https://wandb.ai/thanh-workspace/CL4IE}.}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} continual learning, catastrophic forgetting, document
understanding, key-information extraction, LayoutLMv3, multimodal representation.
}
