\abstractpage{
Deployed document-understanding systems must absorb new document types, schemas, and
domains over time without forgetting what they already know, and they usually cannot keep
the raw documents they were trained on. Multimodal document encoders such as LayoutLMv3
meet the demand for multimodal understanding by fusing text, layout, and visual pathways
into a single network; that network is architecturally heterogeneous, and it remains
unknown \emph{which} part of it drives catastrophic forgetting. This gap matters because
prevailing continual-learning methods are blind to such structure: they regularise the
network uniformly or freeze layers by hand-tuned heuristics, while modern parameter-efficient
and modular adaptation implicitly presupposes knowing where to intervene. The setting adds
two further difficulties: key-information extraction is dense token-level sequence labelling
rather than the image or sentence classification most continual-learning methods were built
for, and document features are close to full rank, so a memory of past tasks cannot simply be
compressed into a small summary.

This thesis studies a stream of key-information-extraction tasks (scanned documents given as
page image, OCR tokens, and bounding boxes, with BIO entity labels) under class-incremental,
domain-incremental, and mixed regimes, learned sequentially by a pre-trained tri-modal encoder
with a bounded memory and no access to past raw data; the output is a single model that
extracts entities from every task seen so far. It asks two questions. \emph{Where} in the
encoder (text, layout, or visual stream, shared layers by depth, or the classifier head) does
forgetting concentrate, and is that locus specific to multimodal fusion or general to the
architecture? And, given the locus, \emph{what} must a memory-bounded remedy supply there, and
\emph{which} stored evidence should it re-expose for a given incoming document?

The diagnosis comes first: a per-component protocol masks the input modalities of a single
LayoutLMv3, holding the architecture fixed, and reads forgetting out through linear CKA drift,
per-group Fisher information, and Fisher-weighted parameter displacement at every task
boundary, contrasted with LiLT, BROS, and a text-only BERT. Forgetting is \emph{output-side}:
the head carries more than an order of magnitude more Fisher importance than any other group
and CKA drift rises monotonically with depth (from $1.00$ at the input encoders to $0.25$ at
the late layers and $0.16$ at the head). The locus is \emph{architecture-general}, recurring on
every backbone including the unimodal one, and it is \emph{conserved}: freezing the diagnosed
head and late layers does not remove the drift but moves it to whatever stays plastic.

A falsification chain then tests the remedies the diagnosis rules in or out across a
ten-method suite over three seeds. Regularisation, distillation, and prompt/LoRA methods fail
for one identified reason: the head's output marginal snaps to the newest task and the drift
beneath it cannot be undone, and pre-registered eval-time and marginal-level corrections are
all nulls. Only replay, which re-exercises old targets at the output, closes the gap (average
entity-F1 $\approx 88$ on domain-incremental learning versus the joint oracle's $88.7$), and
what it must preserve is \emph{whole-document consistency}: replaying intact documents works,
while every summary that breaks the binding of feature, position, and label collapses to the
naive level, even when built from real activations. Per-document compressed latent replay
(CoLaR) respects that law, reaching $87.6$ with a $60$\,MB memory, $2.7\times$ smaller than raw
replay. Building on both, this thesis proposes \textbf{lexically-routed latent replay}: the
drift-immune OCR-vocabulary signature of LexSlot, a slot memory placed at the diagnosed locus
(average entity-F1 $87.3$ with a compact 200-exemplar buffer), serves as the gate that decides
\emph{which} stored document latents are replayed for the incoming document. The thesis
releases the diagnostic protocol, the benchmark, and the tooling as an instrument for designing
continual-learning methods for multimodal document encoders.\footnote{Source code:
\url{https://github.com/hoangthanh283/doccl} (\texttt{doccl} branch); experiments tracked on
Weights \& Biases at \url{https://wandb.ai/thanh-workspace/doccl-aaai2027}.}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} continual learning, catastrophic forgetting, document
understanding, key-information extraction, LayoutLMv3, latent replay.
}
