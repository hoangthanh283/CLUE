\abstractpage{
Deployed document-understanding systems must absorb new document types, schemas, and
domains over time without forgetting what they already know, and they usually cannot keep
the raw documents they were trained on. Multimodal document encoders such as LayoutLMv3
meet the demand for multimodal understanding by fusing text, layout, and visual pathways
into a single network; that network is architecturally heterogeneous, and it remains
unknown \emph{which} part of it drives catastrophic forgetting. Prevailing continual-learning
methods are blind to such structure: they regularise the network uniformly or freeze layers
by hand-tuned heuristics, while parameter-efficient and modular adaptation presupposes
knowing where to intervene. Two further difficulties make the problem hard: key-information
extraction is dense token-level sequence labelling, not the classification most
continual-learning methods were built for, and document features are close to full rank, so
past tasks cannot be compressed into a small summary.

This thesis studies a stream of key-information-extraction tasks (scanned documents given as
page image, OCR tokens, and bounding boxes, with BIO entity labels) under class-incremental,
domain-incremental, and mixed regimes, learned sequentially by a pre-trained tri-modal encoder
with a bounded memory and no access to past raw documents; the output is a single model that
extracts entities from every task seen so far. It asks \emph{where} in the encoder forgetting
concentrates, and, given that, \emph{what} a memory-bounded remedy must store and \emph{which}
stored evidence it should re-expose. The answer is one method,
\textbf{lexically-routed latent replay}, whose three design choices are each fixed by a
measurement: \emph{where} it acts comes from a diagnosis, \emph{what} it stores comes from
testing why other remedies fail, and \emph{which} documents it replays is decided by a lexical
gate.

\emph{Where.} A per-component diagnosis masks the input modalities of a single LayoutLMv3 and
reads forgetting out through linear CKA drift, per-group Fisher information, and
Fisher-weighted parameter displacement at every task boundary, with LiLT, BROS, and a
text-only BERT as contrasts. Forgetting is \emph{output-side}: the classifier head carries more
than an order of magnitude more Fisher importance than any other group, and CKA drift rises
monotonically with depth (from $1.00$ at the input encoders to $0.25$ at the late layers and
$0.16$ at the head). The site is architecture-general, recurring on every backbone including
the unimodal one, and conserved: freezing the head and late layers does not remove the drift
but moves it to whatever stays plastic. The method therefore re-supplies old-task evidence at
the head and late layers rather than protecting parameters.

\emph{What.} Across a ten-method suite over three seeds, regularisation, distillation, and
prompt/LoRA methods fail for one reason: the head's output marginal snaps to the newest task
while the representation beneath it drifts, and pre-registered eval-time and marginal-level
corrections recover nothing. Only replay closes the gap (average entity-F1 $\approx 88$ on
domain-incremental learning versus the joint oracle's $88.7$), and only if it keeps
\emph{whole documents}: every summary that breaks the binding of feature, position, and label
collapses to the naive level, even when built from real activations. The method's memory is
therefore CoLaR, which stores each past document as its own low-rank factors at an
intermediate layer; it reaches $87.6$ with a $60$\,MB memory, $2.7\times$ smaller than raw
replay, without keeping any raw page.

\emph{Which.} Instead of sampling that memory uniformly, the method routes it: each stored
document keeps an OCR-vocabulary signature, which depends only on the input and so cannot
drift, and replay draws the stored documents whose signature matches the incoming batch. The
signature was first validated as the gate of LexSlot, a slot memory at the same site
(average entity-F1 $87.3$ with a compact 200-exemplar buffer, forgetting less than plain
replay). The thesis releases the diagnostic protocol, the benchmark, and the tooling as an
instrument for designing continual-learning methods for multimodal document
encoders.\footnote{Source code: \url{https://github.com/hoangthanh283/doccl} (\texttt{doccl}
branch); experiments tracked on Weights \& Biases at
\url{https://wandb.ai/thanh-workspace/doccl-aaai2027}.}

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} continual learning, catastrophic forgetting, document
understanding, key-information extraction, LayoutLMv3, latent replay.
}
