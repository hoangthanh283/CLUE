\section{Background and Context}
\label{sec:intro-background}

Modern organisations process a continuous stream of \emph{visually-rich
documents}---forms, invoices, receipts, contracts, and reports---whose meaning
depends not only on the words they contain but on \emph{where} those words sit on
the page. Extracting structured information from such documents is the task of
\gls{kie}, the cornerstone of document-understanding systems deployed in finance,
logistics, healthcare, and public administration. The dominant approach casts
\gls{kie} as token-level sequence labelling over a tri-modal input: text obtained
by \gls{ocr}, the two-dimensional layout encoded as bounding boxes, and the page
image. Multimodal document encoders such as LayoutLMv3~\citep{huang2022layoutlmv3},
LiLT~\citep{wang2022lilt}, and BROS~\citep{hong2022bros} fuse these signals and
define the state of the art.

Deployed document systems are not static. New document types appear, annotation
schemas are extended, and the distribution of incoming documents drifts as an
organisation enters new markets or adopts new templates. A model must therefore
\emph{evolve}---learning new entity types and new domains over time---without
re-training from scratch on all historical data, which is often impossible because
earlier data is unavailable, expensive to store, or restricted for privacy reasons
(\gls{pii} under \gls{gdpr}). This is the setting of \gls{cl}: learning a sequence
of tasks while retaining performance on those seen earlier.

The central obstacle to \gls{cl} is \gls{cf}~\citep{mccloskey1989catastrophic}:
when a neural network is fine-tuned on a new task, gradient updates overwrite the
parameters that encoded earlier tasks, and performance on those tasks collapses. A
large literature has developed remedies---regularising important
weights~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc}, distilling from past models~\citep{li2017lwf},
replaying stored exemplars~\citep{rolnick2019er,buzzega2020der}, learning
task-specific prompts~\citep{wang2022l2p,wang2022dualprompt,smith2023coda}, or
allocating low-rank adapters per task~\citep{wang2023olora}. Almost all of these
methods, however, were developed and evaluated on \emph{unimodal} image- or
text-classification benchmarks, and they treat the network as a homogeneous block:
regularisation is applied uniformly across parameters, or layers are frozen by
hand-tuned heuristics.

For a \emph{tri-modal} document encoder this uniform treatment is unsatisfying,
because the network is manifestly heterogeneous: it contains a text stream, a
visual/patch stream, layout (2D position) embeddings, and cross-modal fusion
layers that combine them. These components are trained on different signals, carry
different inductive biases, and---there is every reason to suspect---are unlikely
to age at the same rate under a shifting task distribution. Yet the prevailing
remedies are blind to this structure: elastic-weight
penalties~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc} apply one importance-weighted quadratic term
across all parameters, distillation~\citep{li2017lwf} matches whole-network
outputs, and heuristic layer freezing draws an arbitrary depth-wise cut that
ignores the modality a layer serves. Even the modern parameter-efficient and
modular families---low-rank adapters~\citep{wang2023olora} and prompt
pools~\citep{wang2022l2p,wang2022dualprompt,smith2023coda}---only sharpen the
question rather than answer it: deciding \emph{which} weights to adapt or
\emph{where} to inject a prompt implicitly presupposes that one already knows which
part of the encoder is at risk. Evidence from unimodal vision transformers shows
that forgetting is far from uniform across depth and is concentrated in identifiable
layers~\citep{ramasesh2021anatomy}, and recent probes of multimodal models report
modality-dependent degradation~\citep{zhai2023investigating}; but for a document
encoder that jointly reasons over text, layout, and pixels, \emph{no prior work has
measured which component actually forgets}. That measurement is the missing
prerequisite this thesis supplies.

Answering that question---which component forgets most, and whether the pattern is
stable across continual scenarios---is therefore a prerequisite for designing a
\gls{cl} method that spends its capacity where forgetting actually happens rather
than spreading it thinly across the whole network. This thesis takes a
\emph{measure-first, then-fix} stance: it first \emph{diagnoses} where forgetting
lives in a multimodal document encoder, and only then proposes a remedy whose
design is justified by the diagnosis.

\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:intro-objectives}

The thesis pursues the following concrete objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Formulate continual document \gls{kie} as a unified problem.} Cast
        \gls{kie} across heterogeneous datasets (FUNSD, CORD, SROIE) as continual
        token-level sequence labelling, and define three continual
        scenarios---\gls{cil}, \gls{dil}, and a mixed scenario---over a shared
        output format (Chapter~\ref{ch:experimental-setup}).
  \item \textbf{Build a per-component forgetting diagnostic.} Develop an
        instrumentation protocol that attributes forgetting to individual
        components of a tri-modal encoder using representational drift (linear
        \gls{cka}), per-parameter-group \gls{fim}, and standard forgetting metrics,
        under a controlled modality-ablation design
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}, \S\ref{sec:method-diagnostic}).
  \item \textbf{Characterise where forgetting concentrates.} Apply the diagnostic
        to naive sequential fine-tuning and test whether forgetting is uniform
        across components or concentrates in specific ones, establishing the
        stability of any finding across seeds and task orders
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}, \S\ref{sec:results-diagnosis}).
  \item \textbf{Derive and evaluate a mechanism-targeted remedy.} Propose a
        \gls{cl} method whose mechanism is concentrated on the dominant-forgetting
        component identified by the diagnosis, and evaluate it against ten
        baselines spanning the major method families
        (Chapters~\ref{ch:methodology} and~\ref{ch:results}).
  \item \textbf{Establish a reproducible benchmark.} Release the FCS-CL benchmark
        (FUNSD/CORD/SROIE continual scenarios), the diagnostic tooling, and all
        configurations, with results reported as means over three seeds with
        statistical tests (Chapters~\ref{ch:implementation}
        and~\ref{ch:experimental-setup}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Achievements}
\label{sec:intro-achievements}

% Numeric claims marked TODO are filled once experiments complete (Chapter 6).

\noindent\emph{Contributions at a glance.} The thesis makes three contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a \textbf{per-component forgetting diagnostic} for tri-modal document
        encoders, realised through controlled modality ablation of a single
        backbone and read out by representational drift, per-group \gls{fim}, and
        standard forgetting metrics;
  \item \textbf{DocCL}, a mechanism-targeted continual-learning remedy whose design
        is \emph{derived from} the diagnosis rather than chosen \emph{a priori},
        concentrating its mechanism on the dominant-forgetting component;
  \item the \textbf{FCS-CL benchmark} together with reproducible per-component
        tooling---unified scenarios, baselines, configurations, and
        instrumentation---to support like-for-like comparison.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The remainder of this section expands each contribution.

\noindent\textbf{A per-component forgetting diagnosis for tri-modal document
encoders.} We introduce, to our knowledge, the first protocol that decomposes
\gls{cf} in a multimodal document encoder into its text, visual, layout, and fusion
contributions. By masking input modalities of a \emph{single} LayoutLMv3
architecture we isolate each modality's contribution without the confound of
comparing different architectures. The diagnosis returns a clear, somewhat
counter-intuitive answer: forgetting is \emph{not} a fusion-specific effect but an
\emph{output-side} one, concentrating in the classifier head and the late encoder
layers. Per-group Fisher importance is dominated by the head by roughly an order of
magnitude ($2.2\times10^{-1}$ versus $\le 1.1\times10^{-2}$ for every other group),
and per-layer representational drift (CKA) rises monotonically with depth---from
$1.00$ at the input encoders to $0.22$ at the head---while the input encoders barely
move. A secondary finding is that the text stream is load-bearing: removing it
destabilises training entirely.

\noindent\textbf{A controlled benchmark of continual-learning strategies for document
IE.} We evaluate six core continual-learning strategies---naive and joint bounds,
regularisation (EWC), distillation (LwF), and replay (ER, DER++)---across three
scenarios (class-, domain-, and mixed-incremental) over three seeds under one fixed
configuration. The ordering is unambiguous and consistent with the diagnosis:
replay\,$\gg$\,regularisation\,$\gg$\,distillation. Because forgetting is output-side,
\emph{replay}---which directly re-exposes old-class targets at the head---is the most
effective remedy: on domain-incremental learning both ER and DER++ reach the joint
upper bound (AA $\approx 88$ vs $85$, backward transfer $\approx 0$), closing the
entire $\sim$47-point forgetting gap; EWC roughly halves forgetting where a domain
shift is present; and LwF gives essentially no benefit in this dense
token-classification setting.

\noindent\textbf{A mechanism-targeted continual-learning method (DocCL), designed from
the diagnosis.} Guided by the diagnosis, we design a method that would concentrate its
\gls{cl} mechanism on the dominant-forgetting component (the head and late layers)
rather than treating the network uniformly. Because the diagnosis was inconclusive as
to which \emph{architectural} lever dominates---the signal points at the head, not at
fusion, layout-position, or modality-routing specifically---we follow a pre-registered
fallback and report the diagnosis and benchmark as the primary contribution, leaving
the full empirical evaluation of DocCL as the immediate next step
(Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions}).

\noindent\textbf{The FCS-CL benchmark and reproducible tooling.} We provide a
unified \gls{cl} benchmark over three public document datasets with three
scenarios, a baseline suite, and per-component instrumentation, all under a single
configuration system and tracked experiment log, enabling like-for-like comparison
that the fragmented prior literature has lacked.

\section{Overview of the Dissertation}
\label{sec:intro-overview}

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:background}} reviews document understanding and \gls{ie},
the \gls{cl} problem and its method families, the nascent literature on \gls{cl}
for \gls{ie}, evidence on where forgetting lives in multimodal models, and the
backbones relevant to this work; it closes by identifying the research gaps the
thesis addresses.
\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}} presents the two methodological
contributions: the per-component forgetting diagnostic and the mechanism-targeted
\gls{cl} method derived from it.
\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:implementation}} describes the framework, the
document-\gls{ie} pipeline, and the system architecture, including the
instrumentation hooks and experiment tracking.
\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:experimental-setup}} details the datasets, the continual
task construction, the model and training configuration, the baselines, and the
evaluation methodology.
\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:results}} reports the diagnosis as the headline result,
tests it for stability across seeds and task orders, compares the remedy against
all baselines, presents ablations and a computational-cost analysis, and discusses
implications and threats to validity.
\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions}} states limitations, conclusions, and future
work.
