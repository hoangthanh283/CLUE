\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:concl-limitations}

This study is deliberately scoped, and several limitations should be read
alongside its findings. First, the controlled diagnosis centres on a primary
backbone, LayoutLMv3-base, but its central claim, that forgetting concentrates at
the head and late layers, has been confirmed on three further architectures: the
unimodal BERT baseline and the LiLT and BROS secondary backbones all reproduce the
same head-dominant displacement and depth gradient, and a permutation test cannot
separate their forgetting profiles from LayoutLMv3's ($p=0.38$, $0.51$). What stays
scoped to LayoutLMv3 is now narrower than at first writing: the full retention sweep has
since been completed on the text-only BERT backbone (three seeds on CIL-CORD, DIL, and
Mixed), where the replay-dominant ordering reproduces (\S\ref{sec:results-backbone}), and
partially on LiLT (classical methods on DIL and Mixed); its extension to BROS and the
remaining LiLT cells is a compute-bound follow-up; the cross-backbone \emph{amount} of forgetting is also left open, as the
three-seed $|\mathrm{BWT}|$ comparison is underpowered, so the generalisation claim
is about the \emph{location} of forgetting rather than its magnitude. A second
architectural limit is orthogonal: because LayoutLMv3 is single-stream, the analysis
localises forgetting by depth, input stream, and head rather than by a separable
visual-attention or fusion component, which that architecture does not provide. A
genuinely per-stream \emph{attention} decomposition would require a two-stream
backbone and is left to future work.
Second, the task sequence comprises three public datasets (FUNSD, CORD, SROIE);
while they span forms and receipts and a wide range of label-space sizes, they are
modest in scale and do not cover every document genre, and the unified DIL schema
is VALUE-dominant (KEY present only in FUNSD/SROIE), which is why DIL results are
reported with per-class F1. Third, the modalities' contribution is read
behaviourally from the input-masking conditions (C1--C4) and against an external
unimodal BERT baseline; the within-architecture conditions cannot, on their own,
attribute forgetting to attention sub-blocks the single-stream encoder shares.
Fourth, the work targets token-level key-information extraction and does not
address entity linking, relation extraction, or generative document IE. Fifth, all
results use the OCR transcriptions and bounding boxes supplied with the benchmark
datasets and never re-run a recogniser; this is a deliberate choice that removes OCR
quality as a confounder for the diagnosis, but it also means the findings are
established under clean, dataset-provided text and layout. A deployed pipeline would
introduce its own OCR engine, whose recognition and layout-detection errors differ
across document genres and could interact with the per-component forgetting picture;
quantifying that interaction is left to future work. Sixth, each task is trained to
convergence by early stopping on a validation signal, but FUNSD and SROIE provide no
native validation split, so for those datasets the task's evaluation set serves as both
the stopping signal and the reporting set, a mild optimistic selection bias, bounded by
the short patience and the same rule applied to every method, and disclosed in
\S\ref{sec:exp-checkpointing}; a held-out split carved from training data would remove it
and is a camera-ready refinement. Seventh, the heavier baselines (the 2025 methods and the prompt/LoRA family) were not run on the
large-scale WildReceipt scenario, where only the classical methods and the oracle are
reported; every other baseline cell (including the depth-targeting probe and the
cross-lingual scenario) is measured over three seeds, and the qualitative conclusions do
not depend on that one missing cell. Eighth, and most consequentially for the proposed method,
LexSlot is at present evaluated on the domain-incremental scenario (three seeds) on LayoutLMv3
only; its multi-backbone and other-scenario confirmation in that configuration is the pending
grid (the completed BERT slice runs the buffer-free soft-sharing configuration, which lands at
the naive level there, consistent with the finding that isolation plus the compact buffer,
not the slot mechanism alone, carries the protection
(\S\ref{sec:results-lexslot}), so it does not yet confirm the headline configuration off
LayoutLMv3),
and its lexical gate is expected to be ineffective on class-incremental learning, where
same-dataset sessions collapse the signature contrast the gate relies on, so its claims are
deliberately confined to the domain-shift setting, which is why we frame it as promising rather
than established. Ninth, lexically-routed latent replay is evaluated on the
domain-incremental scenario and LayoutLMv3 at two memory sizes, so its transfer to other
scenarios and backbones is untested. Finally, results rest on three
seeds; more seeds would tighten the confidence intervals around the smaller
effects.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:concl-conclusions}

This thesis studied a stream of key-information-extraction tasks learned sequentially by a
pre-trained tri-modal document encoder, under class-incremental, domain-incremental, and mixed
regimes, with a bounded memory and no access to past raw documents. It asked two questions:
\emph{where} in the encoder forgetting concentrates (RQ1), and \emph{what} a memory-bounded
remedy must supply at that site, and \emph{which} stored evidence it should re-expose (RQ2).

\paragraph{RQ1: where forgetting lives.} A per-component diagnostic (controlled modality
masking of a single LayoutLMv3, read out by per-token CKA drift, per-group Fisher information,
and old-task-Fisher-weighted displacement, and repeated on LiLT, BROS, and a text-only BERT)
gives three answers. Forgetting is \emph{output-side}: under naive sequential fine-tuning an
earlier task that scores $88$ entity-F1 collapses to $\sim$2 F1 after one further task, the head
carries more than an order of magnitude more Fisher importance than any other group, and CKA
drift rises monotonically with depth (from $1.00$ at the input encoders to $0.25$ at the late
layers and $0.16$ at the head). It is \emph{architecture-general}: every backbone, the unimodal
one included, reproduces the site, so it is not a multimodal artefact; because LayoutLMv3 is
single-stream we do not claim a fusion-specific effect. And it is \emph{conserved}: freezing the
diagnosed head and late layers leaves naive-level forgetting (BWT $-76.8$) and moves the drift
into the previously stable early and mid layers (CKA $\approx 1.0 \rightarrow 0.19$), while a fixed
consolidation budget is usable only when concentrated on the site ($\sim$85 AA) and under-fits
when spread uniformly ($42.6$ AA).

\paragraph{RQ2: what the remedy must supply.} The benchmark ordering follows the diagnosis:
replay $\gg$ regularisation $\gg$ distillation. Replay re-exercises old targets at the head and
reaches the joint upper bound on domain-incremental learning (AA $\approx 88$ vs the oracle's
$88.7$, BWT $\approx 0$); EWC curbs forgetting under a domain shift at an accuracy cost; LwF and
the prompt/LoRA families track the naive bound. The buffer-free methods fail for one reason: the
head's output marginal on old tasks snaps to the newest task's label marginal while the
representation beneath it drifts, and pre-registered eval-time and marginal-level corrections
recover nothing, because the old-class probability mass is destroyed before any correction can
act. What replay must then preserve is \emph{whole-document consistency}: replaying intact
documents retains $87.3$ AA, while every summary that breaks the binding of feature, position,
and label (a rank-16 projection, per-class Gaussians, even real $k$-means centroids) collapses to
the naive level. CoLaR, which stores each document as its own rank-$r$ SVD factors, respects that
law and reaches $87.6$ AA with a $60$\,MB memory, $2.7\times$ smaller than raw replay, without
retaining any raw page.

\paragraph{RQ2: which evidence to re-expose.} LexSlot, an isolated slot memory at the diagnosed
site, approaches replay-level accuracy on domain-incremental learning (AA $87.3 \pm 1.0$, BWT
$-2.3$ versus $-2.8$/$-3.4$ for plain replay) with a compact 200-exemplar buffer; its lasting
element is the drift-immune \gls{ocr}-vocabulary signature that gates it. Lexically-routed
latent replay joins that gate to CoLaR's memory: for each incoming document it replays the stored
latents chosen by lexical similarity rather than uniformly, with the memory and loss held fixed so
that any change is the gate's alone. \todo{state the routing verdict under the pre-registered
$\pm1$-point rule, with the $d{=}50$ and $d{=}5$ numbers.}

Taken together, the diagnosis decides \emph{where} a remedy attaches, the falsification decides
\emph{what form} its memory must take, and the lexical gate decides \emph{what} it replays. The
diagnostic protocol, the benchmark, and the tooling stand independently of the remedy, as an
instrument for designing continual-learning methods for multimodal document encoders that the
prior, fragmented literature lacked.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:concl-future}

The convergence-trained grid is now complete, and the measured result is that
the head/late-targeted LexSlot approaches replay with a compact 200-exemplar buffer on the domain shift, forgetting
less than the plain replay methods, competitive with but not beating the strongest replay
variant, while the depth-targeting probe and LexSlot's placement ablation support
H\textsubscript{target} and H\textsubscript{head}. The
benchmark now spans five scenarios, including large-scale class-incremental learning
(WildReceipt) and cross-lingual domain-incremental learning (XFUND); the cross-lingual
result, near-zero forgetting under pure language shift (naive BWT only $-6.6$), further
corroborates the output-side thesis. Beyond that, several extensions
follow naturally and are reserved for post-thesis work; each is a future direction
rather than part of the present AAAI-phase scope.

\emph{Scaling and broadening the diagnosis.} The controlled diagnosis is
established for an encoder-only, base-scale backbone. A natural next step is to
re-run the same per-component protocol on larger and architecturally different
backbones: LayoutLMv3-large, the multilingual LayoutXLM, and decoder-based or
instruction-tuned document MLLMs such as InternVL3, to test whether the
dominant-forgetting site is a property of the LayoutLMv3-base design or a more
general feature of tri-modal document encoders. A particularly important extension
is to a \emph{two-stream} backbone (for example LiLT, whose text and layout streams
are physically separate parameter blocks): there the per-stream \emph{attention}
decomposition that a single-stream encoder cannot support becomes identifiable, so
the modality-partitioned mechanisms set aside in \S\ref{sec:method-design-space}, component-banked
LoRA and modality-routed prompts, could be evaluated on signals that genuinely
exist. Decoder-based encoders are especially interesting because their generative
objective and tokenisation differ markedly from the sequence-labelling setup used
here.

\emph{Additional continual scenarios.} The three studied scenarios cover
class-incremental and domain-incremental shifts. Future work could add
task-incremental learning, where a task identifier is available at inference, and
combined class-and-domain shifts that vary the label space and the document genre
simultaneously; both would stress the components differently and test whether a
single mechanism-targeted remedy transfers across regimes.

\emph{Cross-lingual continual document IE.} The benchmark now includes a
cross-lingual domain-incremental scenario over XFUND
(de\,$\rightarrow$\,es\,$\rightarrow$\,fr\,$\rightarrow$\,it\,$\rightarrow$\,zh,
\S\ref{sec:exp-extended}) and a larger-scale class-incremental scenario over
WildReceipt, so language shift and corpus scale are now first-class axes rather than
gaps. The open question these enable, left for deeper analysis, is whether the
per-component diagnosis itself shifts under language change: when script and
vocabulary change while layout statistics stay comparatively stable, does forgetting
move toward the text pathway, or does it remain output-side as in the English
setting? Running the full per-component diagnostic (CKA, per-group Fisher) inside the
cross-lingual scenario, and extending it to a wider language set, is the natural
continuation.

\emph{Richer, layout-aware remedies.} The diagnosis points to a specific component;
a complementary direction is to enrich the remedy on the data side with a
document-aware structural replay that rehearses not raw tokens but layout-structured
exemplars (for example region- or block-level representations), exploiting the very
spatial structure that distinguishes document IE from plain-text IE. Such a replay
could be combined with the mechanism-targeted approach rather than replacing it.

\emph{Joint multi-task continual IE.} Finally, learning token-level extraction
together with relation extraction (and, eventually, entity linking) in one
continual framework is an open and high-value direction: it would move the
benchmark from single-task sequence labelling towards the joint document
understanding that downstream applications ultimately require.

These extensions are intentionally outside the present scope, which prioritises a
rigorous diagnosis and a single well-justified, diagnosis-derived remedy over breadth; they
sketch the trajectory for a fuller follow-up study.
