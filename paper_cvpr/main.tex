\begin{abstract}
Document understanding systems must keep learning as new document types and label schemas arrive, yet past documents often cannot be retained. Existing continual-learning methods treat the resulting forgetting as a single problem, and on documents they break down as soon as the label space grows. We show that this failure has two distinct causes. One is drift in the upper layers and the classifier, which a memory of whole past documents corrects. The other is a conflict in how the background class is supervised: once new entity types appear, current and stored documents label the same tokens in contradictory ways, and no memory can resolve it. We address each cause directly. CoLaR replays compressed mid-network activations of whole documents in place of the documents themselves, and a session-blocked readout (SBR) gives every session its own background class and routes tokens without task identity. On class-incremental CORD, where the strongest prior method reaches $\nEr$ average F1, the combination reaches $\nSbr$, and a variant that recalls per-session adapter weights reaches $\nRecall$, on par with joint training on all data. We also show that the standard evaluation protocol for this setting penalises correct predictions, and we propose a corrected one.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Organisations process a continuous stream of visually-rich documents (forms, invoices, receipts, contracts) whose meaning depends on the words and on where they sit on the page. Key-information extraction (KIE) is cast as token-level sequence labelling over text, 2-D layout and the page image, and multimodal encoders such as LayoutLMv3~\citep{huang2022layoutlmv3}, LiLT~\citep{wang2022lilt} and BROS~\citep{hong2022bros} define the state of the art. Deployed systems are not static: new document types appear, schemas grow and the input drifts, while retraining on all history is blocked by cost and by privacy constraints on earlier data. This is continual learning (CL), whose central obstacle is catastrophic forgetting~\citep{mccloskey1989catastrophic}.

The CL toolbox (regularisation~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc}, distillation~\citep{li2017lwf}, replay~\citep{rolnick2019er,buzzega2020der}, prompts~\citep{wang2022l2p,smith2023coda}, low-rank adapters~\citep{wang2023olora}) treats forgetting as one phenomenon with one dial between stability and plasticity. On documents that picture produces a puzzle. Under a domain shift with a fixed label space, plain replay recovers joint-training accuracy on every backbone we test. When the label space grows, every method, replay included, sits near the naive level, and a better memory does not help. The usual reading is that class-incremental learning is simply a harder case of the same forgetting.

We show it is a different failure. Continual KIE fails for two \emph{separable} reasons. \textbf{Drift}: the output-facing computation (upper layers and classifier) moves when it is trained on the next task, and any method that keeps updating a shared network without old-task supervision loses the old readout. \textbf{Background conflict}: under label growth a token labelled ``O'' means ``none of the classes seen so far'', so current documents label old entities as background while stored documents label new entities as background, and both push the same logit in opposite directions on the same surface forms. Memory addresses the first and is blind to the second. The standard per-session scoring hides the second as well, because it charges a correct prediction of another session's entity as an error: a jointly trained model scores $33$ under it and $\nJoint$ once each error is charged once.

\Cref{fig:teaser}a is the paper in one picture. On class-incremental CORD a whole-document memory with a shared softmax reaches $\nColar$ and a session-blocked readout without memory reaches $\nNoBank$, both close to naive fine-tuning ($\nNaive$); the two together reach $\vSbr$, against $\vJoint$ for joint training. Each remedy is necessary and neither is sufficient, which is what two independent causes predict.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A corrected protocol for token-level class-incremental evaluation.} Session-restricted scoring charges every error once; we pair it with a mask-free full-label F1, and show that the reported ``floor'' of class-incremental document IE is largely a scoring artefact (\cref{sec:setup}).
  \item \textbf{A separation of two causes of forgetting.} Drift is present in every scenario, shared by four encoders, relocated rather than removed by freezing, and not repaired by five families of shared-network methods; background conflict is specific to label growth and survives a memory with perfect labels. A $2{\times}2$ over memory and readout isolates them (\cref{sec:causes}).
  \item \textbf{SBR and SBR-Recall.} A session-blocked readout gives each session its own background logit and routes every token with no task identity; with a whole-document memory it comes within a few points of joint training on class-incremental CORD and is level with it on a mixed stream. SBR-Recall stores a low-rank trunk delta per session and reads each block under its own delta, which removes drift by construction and needs no memory on a single-schema stream (\cref{sec:sbr,sec:recall}).
  \item \textbf{CoLaR.} A falsification ladder shows that a document memory must keep whole documents, and that each document can be compressed on its own: per-document truncated SVD of mid-network activations matches raw-exemplar replay under domain shift at $60$\,MB (\cref{sec:colar}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Continual-learning families.} Following \citet{wang2024survey}, remedies fall into regularisation (EWC~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc}, SI~\citep{zenke2017si}, MAS~\citep{aljundi2018mas}, LwF~\citep{li2017lwf}), replay (GEM~\citep{lopezpaz2017gem}, ER~\citep{rolnick2019er}, DER++~\citep{buzzega2020der}, iCaRL~\citep{rebuffi2017icarl}), optimisation geometry (GPM~\citep{saha2021gpm}, OGD~\citep{farajtabar2020ogd}, C-Flat~\citep{bian2024cflat}), methods over a frozen backbone (L2P~\citep{wang2022l2p}, DualPrompt~\citep{wang2022dualprompt}, CODA-Prompt~\citep{smith2023coda}, O-LoRA~\citep{wang2023olora}, CL-LoRA~\citep{he2025cllora}, RanPAC~\citep{mcdonnell2023ranpac}) and architectural isolation~\citep{mallya2018packnet,yoon2018den}. Replay is empirically the strongest~\citep{masana2022class,wang2024survey} but stores raw data. Exemplar-free alternatives replay class-conditional feature statistics (PASS, FeTrIL, FeCAM~\citep{zhu2021pass,petit2023fetril,goswami2023fecam}) or merge models and realign the classifier (LCA~\citep{lca2026}).

\paragraph{Class-incremental learning as prediction plus task identification.} \citet{kim2022theoretical} decompose class-incremental learning into within-task prediction and task-identity prediction, and tie the latter to out-of-distribution detection; recent adapter methods train one module per task and infer which to use at test time (EASE~\citep{zhou2024ease}, InfLoRA~\citep{liang2024inflora}). SBR is an instance of this decomposition with one property specific to token-level extraction: every document of a session contains tokens that are true negatives for that session's classes, so the per-session background logit is a task-identity detector trained with real negatives, at no extra cost and per token. In continual NER, ICE~\citep{liu2023ice} trains per-session classifiers on a frozen encoder and SpanKL~\citep{zheng2023spankl} uses per-type sigmoid heads; neither trains the trunk or keeps a memory.

\paragraph{Latent and compressed replay.} REMIND~\citep{hayes2020remind} quantises later-layer features, Latent Replay~\citep{pellegrini2020latent} banks activations at a chosen layer, and generative replay~\citep{vandeven2020brain} samples a per-class model. Each stores one summary per token or class and targets image labels. CoLaR stores one SVD per document, because a token's feature, position and label must stay bound together (\cref{sec:colar}).

\paragraph{Where forgetting lives; continual IE.} \citet{ramasesh2021anatomy} localise forgetting in image classifiers to the layers nearest the output and \citet{zhai2023investigating} report the same for multimodal LLMs; our drift analysis agrees with them and adds the intervention. Continual NER handles the shift of ``O'' with distillation and pseudo-labels (CPFD~\citep{zhang2023cpfd}, CFNER~\citep{zheng2022cfner}) or span generation~\citep{gencner}; continual relation extraction uses episodic memory with prototypes~\citep{han2020emar,cui2021rpcre,zhao2022crl}. All are sentence-level and text-only. Document-level CL is nascent: GDI-Bench~\citep{gdibench} freezes layers heuristically, continual table detection~\citep{minouei2022table} uses plain ER, and ProtoNER~\citep{kumar2024protoner} is few-shot.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Task and scenarios.} A document is a triple of OCR tokens, normalised boxes and the page image; the model emits BIO tags per token and is scored by entity-level F1 (\texttt{seqeval}). We use FUNSD (149/50 train/test forms), CORD (800/100/100 receipts, 30 classes) and SROIE (626/347 receipts). \textbf{DIL}: FUNSD$\rightarrow$SROIE$\rightarrow$CORD under one fixed four-entity schema; only the input distribution shifts. \textbf{CIL-CORD}: CORD's 30 classes arrive six at a time over five sessions; the head grows, and tokens of classes not yet introduced are labelled ``O''. \textbf{Mixed}: six sessions alternating new classes within CORD with new domains (FUNSD, SROIE) and ending in a FUNSD revisit. Details are in the supplement (Sec.~A).

\paragraph{Backbones and methods.} LayoutLMv3-base~\citep{huang2022layoutlmv3} is the primary encoder; LiLT~\citep{wang2022lilt}, BROS~\citep{hong2022bros} and text-only BERT are secondary. Heads are linear. The comparison set is fixed in advance: naive fine-tuning and joint training as references, and one published method per family: ER~\citep{rolnick2019er} and DER++~\citep{buzzega2020der}, LwF~\citep{li2017lwf}, CODA-Prompt~\citep{smith2023coda}, CL-LoRA~\citep{he2025cllora}, LCA~\citep{lca2026}, and the continual-NER methods CPFD~\citep{zhang2023cpfd} and IS3~\citep{chen2024is3}. Replay baselines keep $200$ raw documents (DER++ $\alpha{=}\beta{=}0.5$) unless a row states a matched memory; LwF uses $T{=}2$ and CL-LoRA rank $8$. Our memory keeps $50$ documents per session.

\paragraph{Training protocol.} AdamW, learning rate $5{\times}10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$, gradient clipping $1.0$, batch size $2$, three seeds. Under the \emph{clean protocol} every session trains in full precision for at most $10$ epochs (joint training: $100$) with early stopping, patience $2$, on a $10\%$ split held out from that session's \emph{training} data; the test split is never used for model selection. Cells marked $\dagger$ come from our earlier grid, which early-stops on the session's evaluation split and, for the re-scored baselines, uses fp16 mixed precision. We mark every such cell and do not mix the two protocols within a comparison that the text relies on.

\paragraph{Scoring.} In CIL-CORD and the class-incremental halves of Mixed, session $j$'s test labels show only session $j$'s classes; every other entity is ``O''. Scoring raw predictions against them (the practice we call legacy scoring) charges a model that \emph{correctly} labels another session's entity with a false positive in every other session, so a jointly trained model reads $33$. \emph{Session-restricted scoring} maps a predicted tag outside session $j$'s label set to ``O'' before span matching, without re-taking the argmax among the allowed tags. Each predicted entity is then scored in the one session that owns its class, so an error is charged once and a confusion between sessions still costs a miss. Because a session's test split omits receipts with none of its entities, we also report a mask-free \emph{full-label F1}: the final model on the unmasked CORD test set with all 30 classes. From $R[i,j]$ (F1 on session $j$ after training session $i$) we report average accuracy AA $=\tfrac1T\sum_j R[T,j]$ and backward transfer BWT $=\tfrac1{T-1}\sum_{j<T}(R[T,j]-R[j,j])$.

\section{Two Causes of Forgetting}
\label{sec:causes}

\subsection{Cause 1: drift of the output-facing computation}
\label{sec:drift}
\paragraph{Forgetting is total within one boundary, on every backbone.} Under naive sequential fine-tuning the first task collapses after one further task: FUNSD falls from $89.1$ to $3.3$ on LayoutLMv3, $83.9$ to $2.1$ on LiLT and $88.9$ to $6.6$ on BROS. \Cref{tab:dil} gives the DIL picture on four backbones.

\caption{\textbf{Drift is output-facing, and freezing moves it.} Left: linear CKA between checkpoints before and after the second task falls with depth on all four backbones (1.0 = no drift). Right: freezing the head and late layers leaves backward transfer unchanged, (a) whatever the number of frozen layers and (b) on three DIL task orders and a ten-session CIL-CORD stream (LayoutLMv3, seed 42, no memory).}
\label{fig:conservation}\end{figure*}

\paragraph{Where it sits.} Representations drift cumulatively with depth: per-token CKA~\citep{kornblith2019similarity} between consecutive checkpoints is $1.00$ at the embeddings, $0.78$ over the early layers, $0.34$ in the middle, $0.25$ at the last layer and $0.16$ at the classifier, with the same profile on all four backbones (\cref{fig:conservation}, left) and under LayoutLMv3 modality masks (full, text+layout and text-only models forget alike: BWT $-81$, $-88$, $-79$). The classifier is also where parameters that mattered for the old task are overwritten: old-task-Fisher-weighted displacement is two to four orders of magnitude larger there than in any encoder bucket. That weighting favours the head by construction, and inside the encoder the weighted displacement sits in the early and middle layers, not the late ones, so we read ``late layers'' as a statement about representations only (supplement, Sec.~D). This agrees with the image-classification anatomy of \citet{ramasesh2021anatomy} and is not our contribution; the interventions below are.

\paragraph{Freezing relocates the drift.} Freezing the whole network after the first task gives AA $41.1$ with BWT $0.0$ on DIL: nothing is forgotten and nothing is learned. Freezing only the head and late layers, with the early and middle encoder left plastic, gives AA $38.8$ and BWT $-76.8$, the naive level, while early-layer CKA, ${\approx}1.0$ under naive training, falls to $0.19$. The drift moved into whatever remained plastic (\cref{fig:conservation}, right; seed 42).

\paragraph{Only old-task supervision of the readout repairs it.} Replay reaches joint training on all four backbones; EWC and LwF stay at the naive level (\cref{tab:dil}). We then built or ported five families that keep updating a shared network with no stored old-task targets (head merging, merging with Gaussian classifier realignment~\citep{lca2026}, subspace transfer, isolated head slots, feature-Gaussian replay): head merging is a no-op ($21.6$ against $21.9$ unmerged at its operating point), merging with realignment reaches $43.9$, subspace transfer $39.8$, head slots $42.2$, and feature-Gaussian replay $66.0$ by recovering the densely labelled task only, against $41.3$ for naive and $88$ for replay (supplement, Sec.~C). They fail in one way. The head's output marginal on old tasks snaps to the just-trained task's marginal (cosine $0.997$--$0.998$ against $0.60$ to the old task's own), and classes that are never re-exercised fall to exactly $0.0$ F1. Five post-hoc corrections of that marginal, each with a pass bar fixed before the run, recover at most $-0.1$ AA, because old-class probability mass is ${\sim}10^{-6}$. Our claim is scoped accordingly: on a fixed label space, no method we tried that updates a shared trunk and readout without old-task supervision recovers the old tasks. \Cref{sec:recall} shows the claim does not extend to label growth, where isolation becomes possible.

\paragraph{The size of a remedy's benefit is backbone-dependent; the cause is not.} The gap between joint and naive training on Mixed agrees to $2.5$ points across the four backbones, whereas the benefit of EWC over naive spans $17$ points and changes sign ($-11.9$ on LayoutLMv3, $-2.6$ on LiLT, $+3.5$ on BROS, $+5.2$ on BERT; legacy scoring, supplement Sec.~D). A single-backbone comparison of methods without old-task supervision can land on either side of zero, which is why \cref{tab:dil} reports four.

\subsection{Cause 2: background conflict under label growth}
\label{sec:conflict}
On CIL-CORD under legacy scoring every method sits at $16$--$24$ AA against $33$ for joint training, which has been read as a floor. Session-restricted scoring removes the artefact for the reference, which rises to $\vJoint$, and leaves the methods where they were: ER $\vEr$, CoLaR $\nColar$, naive $\vNaive$ (\cref{tab:main}). The gap that remains is real, and it is not a memory problem.

A document stored at session $t$ carries ``O'' on every entity introduced after $t$, and a current document carries ``O'' on every entity of earlier sessions. With one shared softmax both sets of labels supervise the same background logit, on receipts that look alike, in opposite directions. Improving the memory does not remove the contradiction: refreshing the stored labels with the true current labels, a ceiling no deployable method can reach, gives $61.2$ (seed 42), and restricting the replay loss to the classes alive at storage time gives $42.0$ (two seeds). Perfect memory labels recover about half of the gap, because current documents still mislabel old entities. The continual-NER methods designed for the shift of ``O'' do not recover it either (\cref{tab:main}).

\section{Remedies}
\label{sec:remedies}

\subsection{CoLaR: a memory for drift}
\label{sec:colar}
A memory repairs drift by re-exercising the readout on inputs that keep their old labelling. To find the least it must store we vary the stored object and hold the mechanism fixed: activations are captured at layer $k$, the layers below are frozen after the first task, and stored activations are replayed through the plastic upper layers and head (DIL, LayoutLMv3; single-seed diagnostics unless stated).

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/colar_pareto.pdf}
\caption{Memory size versus retention (DIL, log-scale memory). Per-document SVD (CoLaR) matches the raw latent bank at $2.7\times$ less storage; every summary that breaks whole-document binding falls to the naive level.}
\label{fig:pareto}\end{figure}

\paragraph{Whole documents retain; summaries collapse.} Replaying the stored activations of $50$ whole documents per task retains AA $87.3$. Summaries of the \emph{same} store collapse to the naive level: a rank-16 pooled spectral summary $41.9$, per-class Gaussians $39.4$, real $k$-means centroids $36.7$--$39.7$. A single-variable control isolates the ingredient: four whole documents retain $63.8$, and the identical token features placed on layout carriers from other documents retain $36.7$. The binding of a token's feature to its position and its label is worth $27$ points. The requirement is specific to documents. On ViT-B/16 image class-incremental learning (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R; supplement, Sec.~B) a class-conditional Gaussian head (SLCA~\citep{zhang2023slca}, $87.5/65.0$) beats the same latent memory ($80.0/55.1$), and a ridge readout refit on the trained trunk reaches $88.3/72.3$: there the trunk is intact and only readout statistics are stale. An image carries one label, so a per-class statistic summarises its readout; a document carries one label per token bound to a position, and the Gaussian analogue is null on documents in every form we tried ($39.4$ as a store).

\paragraph{Per-document compression.} The pooled feature space is close to full rank, but one document's tokens share a layout and a vocabulary, so its activation matrix $H_j\in\mathbb{R}^{N_j\times d}$ is low-rank. CoLaR stores a truncated SVD per document, $H_j\approx U_r\Sigma_rV_r^{\top}$, with the document's boxes, attention mask and labels, rebuilds $\hat H_j$ at replay, and injects it at layer $k$; the loss is cross-entropy on the current batch plus cross-entropy on replayed documents. At $k{=}4$, $r{=}128$ and $50$ documents per task it retains $\vColarDil$ AA on DIL in $60$\,MB, against $\vErDil$ for ER with $200$ raw documents and $\vJointDil$ for joint training, and is $2.7\times$ smaller than the uncompressed latent bank it matches ($87.3$ at $163$\,MB; \cref{fig:pareto}); $r{=}64$ gives $80.6$ at $32$\,MB. The memory holds no raw pixels or token ids. We make no formal privacy claim: mid-network activations can leak content. Rebalancing the memory (class-balanced loss, coverage-based selection, entity-weighted compression) trades one task for another without moving AA, and read-side additions are negative (supplement, Sec.~C).

\subsection{SBR: a readout for the background conflict}
\label{sec:sbr}
Let session $b$ introduce the tag set $\mathcal{T}_b$. A shared softmax normalises over $\{\mathrm{O}\}\cup\bigcup_b\mathcal{T}_b$. The session-blocked readout gives every session its own background logit $o_b(x)=w_b^{\top}h(x)$ on the token feature $h(x)$ and normalises each block separately:
\begin{equation}
p_b(\cdot\mid x)=\operatorname{softmax}\big([\,o_b(x),\,z_{\mathcal{T}_b}(x)\,]\big),
\label{eq:block}
\end{equation}
where $z$ are the tag logits of the usual linear head. Within block $b$ a token labelled ``O'' in session-$b$ data is truly background, so the block's supervision is never stale. For a token $x$ with label $y$ in a document of session $s$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(x,y,s)=\sum_{b}\,m_{sb}\;\mathrm{CE}\big(p_b(\cdot\mid x),\,y_b\big),\qquad
y_b=\begin{cases} y & y\in\mathcal{T}_b\\ \mathrm{O}_b & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\label{eq:sbr}
\end{equation}
with one rule for the mask: $m_{sb}{=}1$ when session $s$ annotates every tag of $\mathcal{T}_b$ (the block is supervised fully); $m_{sb}{=}1$ with $y_b{=}\mathrm{O}_b$ when $b$ belongs to a different annotation schema, so none of its entities can occur in the document; and $m_{sb}{=}0$ otherwise, because the document's ``O'' labels say nothing about block $b$. Stored documents follow the rule of the session that stored them. A session that adds no tags creates no block, so on a fixed label space SBR has a single block.

Inference needs no task identity. Each block reports how strongly it claims the token, $e_b=1-p_b(\mathrm{O}_b\mid x)$; the block with the largest $e_b$ labels the token with its best tag if $e_b>0.5$, and the token is ``O'' otherwise. The trunk is shared across blocks and kept stable by the CoLaR memory.

\subsection{SBR-Recall: isolation in place of memory}
\label{sec:recall}
With SBR the memory is written during training and never read at test time. SBR-Recall also stores, for each session $b>1$, a low-rank delta $\Delta_b$~\citep{hu2022lora} of the layers above $k$ (rank $64$ on the attention projections and feed-forward layers), freezes the base after the first session, and computes block $b$ under the trunk state that learned it:
\begin{equation}
p_b(\cdot\mid x)=\operatorname{softmax}\big([\,o_b,\,z_{\mathcal{T}_b}\,]\big)\big(x;\,\theta_0+\Delta_b\big).
\label{eq:recall}
\end{equation}
Every term of \cref{eq:sbr} is computed under the delta its block is read under, at training and at test time. Old blocks can then not drift, since neither the base nor their delta is updated, and routing by $e_b$ is unchanged. Inference costs one pass through the upper layers per session.

Recall is the isolation row of \cref{tab:twobytwo}. It works without a memory because label growth supplies what isolation normally lacks, a routing key: each session's background logit, trained on that session's true negatives, tells the model which delta a token belongs to. Where there is no such key, isolation does not replace memory. On a fixed label space there is one block and nothing to route by. Across schemas the all-background terms of \cref{eq:sbr} have no positive counterweight without stored documents: on Mixed, Recall without a memory scores $44.7$ (seed 42), with other-schema blocks silent until their domain returns, and $85.9$ with the $50$-document memory restored.

\paragraph{Ablations.} The blocked readout on a trunk frozen after the first session scores $14.8$ (later sessions cannot be learned), and without a memory $\nNoBank$, so neither the readout nor plasticity alone is the effect. Tuning the claim threshold and a per-block bias on the test set itself changes AA by at most $0.6$, so what SBR still loses to joint training is retention of early sessions (\cref{fig:retention}), which is the part Recall removes. For Recall, rank $8$ on the attention projections alone gives $80.5$ with flat retention but two sessions stuck near $65$ F1: isolation is sound and the capacity of the delta is what CORD's look-alike price classes need. Stacking lexically routed head slots on SBR (LexSlot) leaves the mean within two points on all three scenarios and narrows the spread between seeds on CIL-CORD and DIL; both mechanisms partition the same readout.

\paragraph{Cost.} CoLaR-SBR stores $50$ compressed documents per session ($96$\,MB on CIL-CORD) and one background logit per session. SBR-Recall stores no documents on a single-schema stream and $54$\,MB of half-precision deltas for the four later sessions, and pays one upper-layer pass per session at inference (about $3.7\times$ the compute of one forward pass at five sessions and $k{=}4$).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The study covers three English datasets with test sets of $50$ to $347$ documents and four encoder-only backbones; SBR and SBR-Recall are evaluated on LayoutLMv3. Cells marked $\dagger$ early-stop on the evaluation split and are not compared against clean-protocol cells in the text. The freezing experiment and the memory-content ladder are single-seed diagnostics; the tables are three-seed unless a cell shows fewer values. Joint training is a reference, not a bound: with early stopping it can be matched or slightly exceeded. SBR-Recall grows by one delta and one inference pass per session, freezes its base on the first session, so its accuracy can depend on which session comes first, and does not replace memory on a fixed label space or across annotation schemas; its Mixed result is one seed. SBR's supervision rule uses the annotation schema of each session, which is known at training time and is not used at test time. CoLaR is a replay method whose memory grows with the number of tasks; its settings were chosen on LayoutLMv3 and transfer to BROS but trail raw replay by $5$--$9$ points on the text-heavy BERT and LiLT. CoLaR stores no raw pixels or token ids, which is weaker than privacy.
