\begin{abstract}
We study catastrophic forgetting issues in document understanding as key-information extraction (KIE) models must adapt to new documents arriving in a streaming fashion, which often leads them to forget what they learned from previous ones. Whereas replaying stored documents shows competitive performance against joint training when new domains arrive, it suffers from a big gap when the label space grows, known as class-incremental learning (CIL). We investigate the cause of this gap. A memory only repairs the drift of the model, and the remaining forgetting mainly comes from the background label ``O'', since a shared softmax treats every ``O'' label as evidence against all classes while it is evidence only against the classes its annotator could see. Based on this finding, we propose a novel method called CoLaR-SBR. Inspired by latent replay and per-session readouts, our method maintains a Compressed Latent Replay (CoLaR) memory of whole documents to remind the model of past sessions, and uses a Session-Blocked Readout (SBR) that gives each session its own background logit to remove the conflict on the ``O'' label. We also show that per-session low-rank updates can replace the memory when all sessions share one annotation schema. We benchmark our method against various continual learning baselines on 5 public document datasets and achieve significant improvement in the class-incremental setting, with competitive performance against the upper-bound baseline Joint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The recent literature has shown the high potential of multimodal encoders such as LayoutLMv3~\citep{huang2022layoutlmv3}, LiLT~\citep{wang2022lilt} and BROS~\citep{hong2022bros} in key-information extraction (KIE), where each OCR token of a visually-rich document (form, invoice, receipt) is labelled using its words, its 2-D layout and the page image. However, when documents arrive in a streaming fashion, with new document types and growing label sets, these models suffer from catastrophic forgetting~\citep{mccloskey1989catastrophic}. Retraining on all history is often blocked by cost and by privacy constraints. Thus, a deployed KIE model has to learn continually.

There exist many continual learning (CL) methods to cope with catastrophic forgetting, and replay is empirically the strongest~\citep{masana2022class,wang2024survey}. On documents, replay looks sufficient at first. When new domains arrive under one fixed label space, i.e., domain-incremental learning (DIL), replaying a few hundred stored documents keeps LayoutLMv3 close to joint training (\cref{app:drift}). Unfortunately, when the label space grows session by session, i.e., class-incremental learning (CIL), replay ends far below joint training (\cref{tab:main}). Our ports of distillation, prompts, low-rank adapters and the continual named-entity recognition (NER) methods designed for the shift of the ``O'' label do no better. It is expected that a class-incremental stream is harder. Nevertheless, it is unclear why a memory that holds the old documents together with their labels does not help. The shift of the background label is known from class-incremental segmentation~\citep{cermelli2020mib,douillard2021plop,cha2021ssul}, incremental detection~\citep{liu2023abr,mo2024bpf} and continual NER~\citep{monaikul2021extendner,zhang2023cpfd,ma2023learning}, and separated or per-session softmaxes~\citep{ahn2021ssil,kim2022more,liu2023ice} already undo the shared normalisation. However, to our knowledge, no prior work examines, on token-labelled documents, which part of the failure a memory repairs and which part remains.

\input{fig/conflict}

In this work, we investigate the cause of this gap and propose a novel method called CoLaR-SBR. First, we examine a ladder of hypotheses on two class-incremental streams, CIL-CORD and CIL-FUNSD (\cref{tab:ladder}). For each hypothesis, we state what it predicts, run an intervention on the same encoder, stream and recipe, and measure where the errors go (\cref{fig:findings}). We find that label growth does not only make forgetting harder (H0). With a whole-document memory, the model suppresses its last sessions, which naive training does not do. Then, on CIL-CORD, we relabel every stored document with its true labels before each session, which is an oracle control. In this case, the shared softmax comes within $\nOraclePreJointGap$ points of joint training on every seed, although the current documents still call old entities ``O'' (H1, H2). Without true labels, a MiB-style unbiased loss~\citep{cermelli2020mib} recovers about half of the full-label gap, whereas giving each session its own background closes most of it, given a memory (H3, \cref{fig:conflict}). These results hint that a memory only repairs the drift of the model, and the remaining forgetting mainly comes from the background label ``O'', since a shared softmax treats every ``O'' label as evidence against all classes, while it is evidence only against the classes its annotator could see.

Based on this finding, we propose CoLaR-SBR (\cref{fig:method}). Our method maintains a Compressed Latent Replay (CoLaR) memory, inspired by latent replay~\citep{hayes2020remind,pellegrini2020latent}, which stores the compressed activations of whole documents together with their labels and the session that wrote them, to remind the model of past sessions. On top of that, we use a Session-Blocked Readout (SBR), inspired by ICE-O~\citep{liu2023ice} and MORE~\citep{kim2022more}, which gives each session its own background logit and its own softmax. We argue that each class should be supervised only with negatives from the documents that annotated it, as in training on disjoint tag sets~\citep{greenberg2018marginal}. Hence, an ``O'' label trains only the blocks of the sessions that annotated the document. Finally, we show that the memory can be replaced in one case (H4). When all sessions share one annotation schema, per-session low-rank updates~\citep{hu2022lora} (SBR-Recall) replace it and reach within $\nRecallJointGap$ points of joint training on every seed, whereas across schemas they fail without a memory (one seed).

We benchmark our method against two references and eight CL baselines on six streams over five public document datasets, FUNSD, CORD, SROIE, WildReceipt and XFUND. On CIL-CORD and CIL-FUNSD, CoLaR-SBR closes most of the gap to joint training on every seed, while on CIL-CORD the continual-NER and replay methods in our comparison do not. It is noted that our readout is not limited to the latent memory. With raw documents instead, the full-label F1 differs by at most $\nErSbrAbsFullMax$ points on every seed, while the latent memory keeps no pixels or token ids. The contributions of our study are as follows: (1) we investigate why replay fails under label growth and find that a memory only repairs drift, while the remaining forgetting comes from the conflict on the background label (\cref{sec:investigation}); (2) we propose a novel method called CoLaR-SBR, which combines a whole-document latent memory with a per-session background readout to address this conflict, and we show when per-session low-rank updates can replace the memory (\cref{sec:design}); (3) we conduct experiments against various CL baselines on five public document datasets (see \cref{tab:main}), with ablations on each component and on the memory cost (\cref{sec:cost}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Document understanding and KIE.} Recent studies on visually-rich documents mainly consist of layout-aware encoders and generative models. LayoutLM~\citep{xu2020layoutlm} adds the 2-D position of every token to a text encoder, and LayoutLMv2~\citep{xu2021layoutlmv2}, DocFormer~\citep{appalaraju2021docformer} and LayoutLMv3~\citep{huang2022layoutlmv3} bring the page image into pre-training. As another example, LiLT~\citep{wang2022lilt} decouples the layout stream from the language, BROS~\citep{hong2022bros} encodes the relative positions between text boxes, and GeoLayoutLM~\citep{luo2023geolayoutlm} pre-trains geometric relations. On the other hand, Donut~\citep{kim2022donut} and UDOP~\citep{tang2023udop} generate the output sequence directly. These models achieve strong results on forms and receipts such as FUNSD~\citep{jaume2019funsd}, CORD~\citep{park2019cord}, SROIE~\citep{huang2019sroie}, WildReceipt~\citep{sun2021sdmgr} and XFUND~\citep{xu2022xfund}. However, they are trained once on all the data, with a classifier sized to a fixed label set, and their pre-training does not protect them when documents arrive in sequence: fine-tuned session by session on CIL-CORD, LayoutLMv3 falls to $\nNaiveFull$ full-label F1, against $\nJointFull$ for joint training (\cref{tab:main}). Furthermore, a session of a stream annotates only its own fields, so every other entity falls under the background label ``O'', which their benchmarks, annotated with the full schema, do not cover. It is noted that a generative model emits no per-token background label, so the conflict we study concerns token classifiers. Hence, we work with layout-aware encoders and study their forgetting (\cref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Continual learning.} Recent studies on catastrophic forgetting mainly consist of regularization, replay and parameter isolation~\citep{wang2024survey}. Regularization methods penalise changes to the weights that mattered for old tasks~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc,zenke2017si,aljundi2018mas} or distil the outputs of the old model~\citep{li2017lwf}. They store no data, but they are reported to fall behind replay in the class-incremental setting~\citep{masana2022class}. Parameter isolation gives each task its own parameters, for instance by pruning~\citep{mallya2018packnet} or by adding a new column per task~\citep{rusu2016pnn}. It forgets nothing by design. Unfortunately, it grows with the tasks and needs to know the task at test time. More recently, methods built on a frozen pre-trained model learn prompts~\citep{wang2022l2p,wang2022dualprompt,smith2023coda} or low-rank adapters~\citep{wang2023olora,liang2024inflora,wu2025sdlora,he2025cllora}, or keep the backbone almost fixed and realign the classifier~\citep{zhang2023slca,mcdonnell2023ranpac}. These methods are mainly designed for image classification, where each sample carries one label and no class plays the role of a background. Hence, it is a non-trivial task to apply them to token-labelled documents: in our comparison, the ports of LwF, CODA-Prompt, CL-LoRA and LCA all stay at or below a memory with a shared softmax in AA on CIL-CORD (\cref{tab:main}).

\paragraph{Replay and latent replay.} Replay stores a small set of past samples and trains on them together with the new data~\citep{rolnick2019er,rebuffi2017icarl}. Its variants also replay the old logits~\citep{buzzega2020der} or use the memory to constrain the gradient~\citep{lopezpaz2017gem,chaudhry2019agem}, and it is empirically the strongest family~\citep{masana2022class}. To reduce the cost of raw samples, latent replay stores intermediate activations and freezes the layers below them: REMIND~\citep{hayes2020remind} keeps quantised features, and \citet{pellegrini2020latent} keep the activations of one layer. Whereas most prior replay mechanisms focus on which samples to store or how to compress them, we investigate another aspect, namely what the stored labels say. Under label growth, a stored document carries ``O'' on every entity outside its session. Thus, a memory can repair the drift of the encoder and still replay wrong negatives (\cref{sec:h1}). Our memory, CoLaR, is a form of latent replay that keeps whole documents.

\paragraph{Background shift.} When each step annotates only its own classes, the background label changes meaning. In class-incremental segmentation, MiB~\citep{cermelli2020mib} lets a background label mean background or any old class in the loss, and the old model's background mean background or any new class in the distillation; PLOP~\citep{douillard2021plop} pseudo-labels the background with the old model, and \citet{park2024mitigating} assign the background label selectively. SSUL~\citep{cha2021ssul} adds an unknown label and replaces the softmax with per-class sigmoid outputs on a frozen backbone. \citet{kalb2022causes} analyse this setting and find that the semantic shift of the background and the bias towards new classes are the main causes of forgetting, and that both appear mainly in the classification layers. Our investigation is in the same spirit on token-labelled documents; in addition, we separate what a replay memory repairs from what survives true stored labels. Incremental detection has named both its stored side~\citep{liu2023abr} and its two-sided form~\citep{mo2024bpf}. In continual NER, the shift of ``O'' is the unlabelled-entity problem~\citep{ma2023learning}, answered by distillation on ``O'' tokens~\citep{monaikul2021extendner,zheng2022cfner}, pseudo-labels~\citep{zhang2023cpfd,zhang2025spt} and a debiased loss for both directions of the shift~\citep{qiu2024is3}. These methods name the shift. However, most of them answer it through the old model, with distillation targets or pseudo-labels that depend on its quality. Our comparison ports CPFD and IS3 from this family and uses a MiB-style loss as a control (\cref{sec:h2}); PLOP, BPF and per-class sigmoid heads are not run (\cref{sec:limitations}). We do not claim the conflict; instead, we separate its stored and current sides with relabelled memories and an unbiased loss and measure what each leaves.

\paragraph{Separated readouts and task identity.} A softmax over old and new classes together biases them, and separating it removes the bias~\citep{ahn2021ssil}; ER-ACE~\citep{caccia2022erace} masks the old classes out of the softmax of incoming samples. A ``none of mine'' output per task turns class-incremental learning into within-task prediction plus task identification~\citep{kim2022theoretical}. MORE~\citep{kim2022more} isolates each task's parameters and trains each head's out-of-distribution output from saved samples; ICE-O~\citep{liu2023ice}, on a frozen encoder with no memory, trains one softmax per session over a constant Other logit. In continual NER, other works avoid the shared ``O'' tag by span-level multi-label prediction~\citep{zhang2023spankl} or by generating the entities~\citep{gencner}. Unlike them, SBR learns one background per session and keeps old blocks trainable from a memory; SBR-Recall shares MORE's isolation, by low-rank trunk deltas, with no saved samples.

\paragraph{Continual document IE.} There have been few works on continual learning for documents. On plain text, continual information extraction covers NER (above) and relation extraction~\citep{han2020emar,cui2021rpcre,zhao2022crl}, which work at sentence level. Vision-and-language models are studied on continual question answering~\citep{srinivasan2022climb,zhang2025clcrossvqa} and instruction tuning~\citep{chen2024sefe,ge2025dmole}, where the output is generated text with no background label, and multimodal LLMs forget under fine-tuning~\citep{zhai2023investigating}. At document level, GDI-Bench~\citep{gdibench} freezes most parameters, continual table detection~\citep{minouei2022table} uses plain ER, ProtoNER~\citep{kumar2023protoner} uses prototypes and AMD-Proj~\citep{sassioui2026amdproj} projects gradients against task subspaces. Recently, \citet{sassioui2026documents} benchmarked layout-aware models by stream regime and found that no method they test consistently preserves old knowledge under label-space shift. However, they decompose forgetting by regime, whereas we decompose it by cause.

\paragraph{Summary.} Overall, the literature offers two halves of an answer. Replay copes with drift, but with a shared softmax it replays stale ``O'' labels. Background-shift methods and separated readouts address the labels, but they keep one softmax, freeze the encoder or isolate the parameters of each task. To our knowledge, no prior work puts the two together on token-labelled documents or measures what each one repairs. This leads to the direction of our method: a whole-document latent memory against drift, and one background per session against the conflict (\cref{sec:design}).

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:setup}
In the following, we first define continual key-information extraction and the streams we study. Then, we describe how a model is scored.

\paragraph{Continual key-information extraction.} A document is its OCR tokens, normalised boxes and page image; the model emits BIO tags per token, scored by entity-level F1 (\texttt{seqeval}). The documents arrive in $T$ sessions, and session $t$ brings a training set annotated with its own tag set $\mathcal{T}_t$. We consider three kinds of stream. In class-incremental learning (CIL), each session annotates new classes only: the head grows, and every entity outside the session, earlier or later, is labelled ``O''. In domain-incremental learning (DIL), the label space is fixed and the domain changes. In a Mixed stream both change, across annotation schemas. After the last session, the model is tested on the classes of all sessions without knowing the session of a document.

\paragraph{Streams.} We consider six streams over five public document datasets, FUNSD~\citep{jaume2019funsd}, CORD~\citep{park2019cord}, SROIE~\citep{huang2019sroie}, WildReceipt~\citep{sun2021sdmgr} and XFUND~\citep{xu2022xfund} (\cref{tab:bench}; sessions in \cref{app:setup}). Appendices A to F are in the supplementary material. CIL-CORD, the stream of every rung: CORD's $30$ receipt classes arrive six at a time over five sessions. DIL: FUNSD$\rightarrow$SROIE$\rightarrow$CORD under one fixed four-entity schema. Mixed: six sessions over three datasets and annotation schemas. CIL-FUNSD: FUNSD's three entity types in two sessions. CIL-WildReceipt: WildReceipt's $24$ entity classes arrive six at a time over four sessions, built as CIL-CORD. DIL-XFUND: five XFUND languages (German, Spanish, French, Italian, Chinese) arrive in turn under the fixed schema of DIL, so the domain shift is the language. LayoutLMv3-base~\citep{huang2022layoutlmv3} is the encoder of every rung; LiLT~\citep{wang2022lilt}, BROS~\citep{hong2022bros} and BERT appear on DIL.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.} Our headline number is the \emph{full-label F1}: the final model on the complete CORD test set with all 30 classes, as in continual NER~\citep{ma2023learning}. Only clean runs record it; against a cell with only AA ($\dagger$, DIL, Mixed) we compare in AA. Retention needs per-session test splits, whose labels show only that session's classes; from $R[i,j]$, the F1 on session $j$ after session $i$, AA $=\tfrac1T\sum_j R[T,j]$. Since raw scoring of such labels charges a model for every correct entity of another session, we score each session \emph{session-restricted}, mapping a predicted tag outside session $j$'s label set to ``O'' before span matching. On every seed both metrics order the main groups of clean runs the same way (\cref{app:setup}).

\section{Why Replay Fails under Label Growth}
\label{sec:investigation}
Every rung runs on CIL-CORD with LayoutLMv3 under the clean protocol (\cref{sec:protocol}) and changes one thing against the run it is compared with, unless stated (H0's memory rows change several). \Cref{tab:ladder} lists the rungs and \cref{fig:findings} draws them. A verdict word is a sign statement over the three seeds, not a significance test (\cref{sec:limitations}): on every seed, the prediction holds (\emph{supports}), fails (\emph{refutes}) or holds in part (\emph{partly}); \emph{does not support} means it does not hold on every seed, \emph{not needed} that the gap closes without the factor, and \emph{open} that only preliminary, cross-protocol evidence exists. The \emph{blocked readout} (\cref{sec:sbr}) gives each session's tags their own background logit and softmax, a \emph{block}; a block's \emph{claim} on a token is one minus its background probability, and the \emph{owning block} of an entity is the block of its gold class. Unless stated, a memory is CoLaR (\cref{sec:memory}): $50$ documents per session stored as rank-$128$ SVDs of their layer-$4$ activations.

\subsection{H0: only harder forgetting}
\label{sec:h0}
We first hypothesize that label growth only makes drift harder. Then the memory that retains under domain shift would still retain here, a raw memory would do better, and the losses would look like naive forgetting, where old sessions are lost and the newest is learned well. However, the results refute it. The CoLaR memory comes within $\nColarDilGapMax$ AA points of joint training on DIL on every seed (means $\nColarDil^\dagger$ against $\nJointDil^\dagger$). On CIL-CORD with a shared softmax it reaches $\vColarFull$ full-label F1 ($\vColar$ AA) against $\vJointFull$ ($\vJoint$). Raw documents do no better (ER, $\nEr$ AA$^\dagger$, below every CoLaR seed), but ER also trains every layer, keeps $200$ documents and is a legacy run (raw documents under the same readout: \cref{sec:ablations}). What separates the memory run from naive forgetting is the session being trained: its newest session ends at $\mColarLastMin$ to $\mColarLastMax$ F1, whereas naive training learns every session to at least $\nNaiveDiagMin$ on every seed. This run also freezes layers $1..4$ after the first session and replays four stored documents per current batch of two; with the same freezing (and, with the memory, the same replay), the blocked readouts with and without the memory learn every session to at least $\nSbrDiagMin$ (per-seed minimum), so lost plasticity does not explain it.

Hence, drift alone does not explain the failure: naive training, with all of its drift, does not suppress the session being trained. Nevertheless, drift is real: on a fixed label space every encoder forgets its earlier domains, and replay, not EWC or LwF, repairs it (\cref{app:drift}).

\subsection{H1: stale labels in the memory}
\label{sec:h1}
We next hypothesize that the stored labels are stale. A document stored at session $t$ carries ``O'' on every entity of another session, earlier or later, so the memory itself calls background what the model must keep or learn; in detection, this staleness was named foreground shift and blamed for replay's failure~\citep{liu2023abr}. If so, true stored labels should close the gap with the shared softmax unchanged. To examine it, we repair the stored labels in three ways. First, \emph{at each boundary}, the documents stored \emph{before} the session just finished receive the true labels of all classes seen so far; the session trained next is never covered. Then, \emph{before each session}, every stored document receives the true labels of all classes in the grown head, so no stored label is stale during training; the current documents still call old entities ``O''. Finally, we mask the same stale tokens in replay instead, removing the false ``O'' without adding a label. All three read labels no continual learner has: diagnostics, not methods.

The hypothesis is partly supported at the boundary and supported before each session. At the boundary, the refresh lifts full-label F1 from $\nColarSeedFull$ to $\nOracleFull$ (means, \nOracleScope), \nOracleShareFull\% of the seed-matched gap, and stays below CoLaR-SBR under both metrics; it cannot reach the session being trained, whose classes are ``O'' in every stored document (\cref{app:results}). Refreshed before each session, the same memory and softmax reach $\vOraclePreFull$ full-label F1, within $\nOraclePreJointGap$ points of joint training on every seed under both metrics and above CoLaR-SBR on every seed (\cref{tab:ladder}). Masking instead reaches $\vOracleMaskFull$: on every seed it lifts CoLaR by \nOracleMaskMinusColarFullMin{} to \nOracleMaskMinusColarFullMax{} points and ends within $\nOracleMaskJointGap$ points of joint training, and relabelling adds \nOraclePreMinusMaskFullMin{} to \nOraclePreMinusMaskFullMax{} more; removing the stale negatives alone recovers most of the gap. As a result, on CIL-CORD true stored labels suffice to explain nearly all of the failure under a shared softmax, on the session being trained as much as on old ones (\nOracleN), though not on CIL-FUNSD (\cref{sec:beyond}); the controls are oracles.

\subsection{H2: the current documents' ``O'' labels}
\label{sec:h2}
We then hypothesize that the current documents are the cause: each labels every entity of an earlier session ``O'', so even a memory with true labels is opposed in every batch by documents that call the same strings background; this is the second side of the conflict named in detection~\citep{mo2024bpf}. If this pull is the cause, a loss that lets a current ``O'' mean ``O or an earlier class'', as MiB does for segmentation~\citep{cermelli2020mib} and marginal-likelihood taggers do offline~\citep{greenberg2018marginal}, should close the gap within a shared softmax. To examine it, we use the never-stale memory of H1, which leaves every current document stale, and two label-free repairs with the memory and the shared softmax. The first is MiB's unbiased cross-entropy~\citep{cermelli2020mib} in both directions: a current ``O'' token matches ``O'' or any earlier-session class, a replayed one ``O'' or any class introduced after storage (no distillation). The second slices each stored document's replay loss to the classes known at storage: later classes are no longer pushed down, but stale tokens still train the shared ``O'' logit.

The results show that the current side is not needed on CIL-CORD, and that a loss repair only partly helps. With true stored labels the gap closes although every current document still calls old entities ``O'' (H1); hence, on CIL-CORD, the current side is not needed; on CIL-FUNSD it is needed (\cref{sec:beyond}). The MiB-style loss lifts full-label F1 to $\vMibFull$, \nMibShareFull\% of the seed-matched gap, above CoLaR and below joint training on every seed under both metrics, and below CoLaR-SBR on every seed. The softmax slice reaches only $\vSlicedFull$ full-label F1 (\nSlicedShareFull\% of the gap), above CoLaR and below the MiB-style loss on every seed under both metrics, and trains unstably (\cref{app:results}). Hence a softmax slice does not recover the oracle's gain, whereas masking the stale tokens, an oracle (H1), recovers most of it.

\subsection{H3: the shared normalisation}
\label{sec:h3}
We hypothesize that the conflict lives in the shared normalisation, one background logit trained by documents that disagree on the background. Then one background logit per session, normalised with that session's tags only, should close most of the gap with no true labels. This is SS-IL's diagnosis of a shared softmax~\citep{ahn2021ssil} with a background added, and ICE-O's readout~\citep{liu2023ice} without its frozen encoder. It needs the memory, since separating labels does not undo drift: without it, the readout should fail the way drift does.

The hypothesis is supported on every seed. With the memory, the blocked readout (CoLaR-SBR, \cref{sec:sbr}) reaches $\vSbrFull$ full-label F1, $\nSbrGapFullMin$ to $\nSbrGapFullMax$ points below joint training per seed, above the memory relabelled at each boundary and below the memory relabelled before each session on every seed, under both metrics. Without the memory, the same readout scores $\vNoBankFull$, and its errors differ: old blocks on a drifted trunk claim far more than their own entities (\cref{app:results}); that is drift, and the memory reduces it. Nevertheless, the blocked readout is neither the only repair nor the best one: true stored labels before each session do better (\cref{sec:h1}), but the blocked readout needs none. The readout does nothing alone, and it is not free: on DIL, with nothing to separate, CoLaR-SBR is below CoLaR (AA $\nSbrDil^\dagger$ against $\nColarDil^\dagger$).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:design}
In this section, we describe CoLaR-SBR (\cref{fig:method}). In the following, we first state the principle behind it. Then, we detail its memory (\cref{sec:memory}), its readout (\cref{sec:sbr}) and a variant that replaces the memory (\cref{sec:recall}).

\paragraph{The principle.} The rungs point at one fact about annotation under label growth, familiar from training on partially annotated data~\citep{greenberg2018marginal}. A token marked ``O'' in session $s$ means ``none of the classes annotated in session $s$'': it is evidence against those classes and silent about every other. However, a shared softmax turns it into evidence against all classes, and a stale stored label makes the same error inside the memory. We therefore state one principle, which the per-session readout implements and H3 supports, partly as an explanation (\cref{sec:ablations}): \emph{supervise each class only with negatives from documents that annotated it, and normalise together only classes that were annotated together.} Three consequences follow: each session needs its own background; the memory must keep whole documents with their labels and the session that wrote them; and each session's background logit, trained on that session's true negatives, is also a detector of its entities, so it can route a token without task identity. We name the resulting method CoLaR-SBR.

\subsection{CoLaR: a whole-document latent memory}
\label{sec:memory}
Our readout (\cref{sec:sbr}) needs stored documents that keep each token's label at its position and the session that wrote it. CoLaR (Compressed Latent Replay), inspired by latent replay~\citep{hayes2020remind,pellegrini2020latent}, keeps whole documents: after the first session layers $1..k$ are frozen, and each stored document's layer-$k$ activations $H_j$ are kept as a rank-$r$ truncated SVD with its boxes, mask, labels and session, rebuilt at replay and injected at layer $k$; it holds no raw pixels or token ids (no privacy claim: activations can leak content). We use CoLaR because it keeps no pixels or token ids (\cref{sec:cost}).

\subsection{SBR: one background per session}
\label{sec:sbr}
SBR (Session-Blocked Readout) is our instantiation of a per-session background readout~\citep{liu2023ice,kim2022more} (\cref{fig:method}b). Every session that brings new tags opens a block $b$ holding those tags $\mathcal{T}_b$. Whereas a shared softmax normalises over $\{\mathrm{O}\}\cup\bigcup_b\mathcal{T}_b$, SBR gives every session its own background logit $o_b(x)=w_b^{\top}h(x)+c_b$ on the token feature $h(x)$ and normalises each block separately:
\begin{equation}
p_b(\cdot\mid x)=\operatorname{softmax}\big([\,o_b(x),\,z_{\mathcal{T}_b}(x)\,]\big),
\label{eq:block}
\end{equation}
where $z(x)$ are the tag logits of the usual linear head and $z_{\mathcal{T}_b}(x)$ its entries for the tags in $\mathcal{T}_b$. Within block $b$ a token labelled ``O'' in session-$b$ data is truly background, so the block's supervision is never stale. For a token $x$ with label $y$ in a document of session $s$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal{L}(x,y,s)=\sum_{b}\,m_{sb}\;\mathrm{CE}\big(p_b(\cdot\mid x),\,y_b\big),\\
y_b=\begin{cases} y & y\in\mathcal{T}_b\\ \mathrm{O}_b & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:sbr}
\end{equation}
with one rule for the mask: $m_{sb}{=}1$ when session $s$ annotates every tag of $\mathcal{T}_b$; $m_{sb}{=}1$ with $y_b{=}\mathrm{O}_b$ when $b$ belongs to a different annotation schema (every token is a negative for $b$, a training-time convention; \cref{app:method}); and $m_{sb}{=}0$ otherwise, because the document's ``O'' labels say nothing about block $b$. Stored documents follow the rule of the session that stored them. Since a session that adds no tags creates no block, SBR has a single block on a fixed label space. At inference each block reports its claim $e_b=1-p_b(\mathrm{O}_b\mid x)$; the block with the largest $e_b$ labels the token with its best tag if $e_b>0.5$, and the token is ``O'' otherwise, as in ICE-O's ``Other unless some session scores above it''. Blocks trained at different times need not produce claims on one scale; hence we measure the routing directly (\cref{fig:findings}b).

\subsection{SBR-Recall: per-session deltas instead of the memory}
\label{sec:recall}
We hypothesize that, without the conflict, the memory is needed only against drift. The task-identity view~\citep{kim2022theoretical} says when it can go: if each block is read under the trunk state that learned it, nothing drifts for it, provided the model can tell which state a token needs. On one schema under label growth each block's background logit supplies that key. Across schemas, however, the all-background terms of \cref{eq:sbr} train every old block towards background on other-schema documents with nothing to pull it back, so isolation should fail without stored documents; a fixed label space has one block, nothing to route by (not run). To examine this hypothesis (H4), SBR-Recall freezes the layers above $k$ after the first session (the base $\theta_0$), stores for each later session $b$ a rank-$64$ delta $\Delta_b$~\citep{hu2022lora} of them ($\Delta_1{=}0$), and computes every term of \cref{eq:sbr} for block $b$, at training and test time, under the trunk state that learned it (\cref{app:method}):
\begin{equation}
p_b(\cdot\mid x)=\operatorname{softmax}\big([\,o_b,\,z_{\mathcal{T}_b}\,]\big)\big(x;\,\theta_0+\Delta_b\big).
\label{eq:recall}
\end{equation}
We report its results in \cref{sec:ablations}.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Experimental setup}
\paragraph{Baselines.} We benchmark against two references (naive fine-tuning, joint training) and eight methods from five families (\cref{tab:main}): ER~\citep{rolnick2019er}, DER++~\citep{buzzega2020der}, LwF~\citep{li2017lwf}, CODA-Prompt~\citep{smith2023coda}, CL-LoRA~\citep{he2025cllora}, LCA~\citep{lca2026}, and the continual-NER methods CPFD~\citep{zhang2023cpfd} and IS3~\citep{qiu2024is3} (\cref{app:baselines}).

\paragraph{Training protocol.}\label{sec:protocol} We use AdamW, learning rate $5{\times}10^{-5}$ (LCA: its own SGD recipe; \cref{app:baselines}), batch size $2$ and seeds 42, 7 and 123; memory runs replay four stored documents per current batch of two. Under the \emph{clean protocol} every session trains in full precision for at most $10$ epochs (joint training: $100$) and early-stops on $10\%$ held out from its \emph{training} data; the test split never selects a model. Cells marked $\dagger$ come from earlier runs (\emph{legacy protocol}) that early-stop on the test split and vary in epoch cap and precision. A cell is clean only when all three clean seeds exist (\cref{app:setup}).

\paragraph{Hyperparameter tuning.} Tuning and evaluating on the same scenario overstates CL methods~\citep{cha2025hyperparameters}. Hence, the comparison set was fixed after a first grid on all three streams, the memory settings were chosen on DIL, SBR's claim threshold ($0.5$) was not tuned, and the one setting chosen on CIL-CORD is SBR-Recall's delta capacity (\cref{app:baselines,app:results}).

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:beyond}
\paragraph{Results on CIL-CORD.} \Cref{tab:main} shows the comparison between our method and the baselines. CoLaR-SBR reaches $\vSbrFull$ full-label F1 ($\vSbr$ AA), against $\vJointFull$ for joint training and $\vColarFull$ for the same memory with a shared softmax. On CIL-CORD every shared-softmax method of \cref{tab:main} stays at or below CoLaR in AA ($\nColar$). All baselines are our ports, none checked against published numbers; four end below naive training on every seed as they learn later sessions poorly (\cref{app:baselines}).

\paragraph{Results on CIL-FUNSD.} On CIL-FUNSD (two sessions, ten stored forms per session; supplementary \cref{tab:funsd}), true stored labels do not rescue the shared softmax: the never-stale memory differs from plain CoLaR by \nFunsdOracleMinusColarFullMin{} to \nFunsdOracleMinusColarFullMax{} full-label points per seed and forgets session~1 ($R[2,1]$ at most $\nFunsdOracleSessOneMax$), and with fifty stored forms it reaches only $\vFunsdOracleFiftyFull$. The MiB-style loss reaches $\vFunsdMibFull$, level with CoLaR-SBR ($\vFunsdSbrFull$) on every seed. Slicing only the current loss lifts CoLaR by \nFunsdCurSlicedMinusColarFullMin{} to \nFunsdCurSlicedMinusColarFullMax{} points but stays below CoLaR-SBR on every seed: the current side is needed, and slicing it alone falls short. The blocked readout keeps session~1 on every seed ($R[2,1]$ at least $\nFunsdSbrSessOneMin$), as does a constant background, and uses the memory: without it, it is below CoLaR-SBR on every seed.

\paragraph{Results on Mixed.} On Mixed, CoLaR-SBR reaches $\vSbrMix$ AA, against $\vJointMix$ for joint training, both under the clean protocol. ER has only a legacy cell ($\nErMix^\dagger$, two seeds), so we do not rank it against the two.

\paragraph{Results on DIL and DIL-XFUND.} On a fixed label space there is one block and nothing to separate. On DIL, every replay method comes close to joint training, and the blocked readout brings no gain: CoLaR-SBR is below CoLaR (AA $\nSbrDil^\dagger$ against $\nColarDil^\dagger$). On DIL-XFUND, CoLaR and CoLaR-SBR improve over naive fine-tuning, but they stay below ER, which keeps raw documents and trains every layer, and below joint training (\cref{tab:main}). The clean CIL-WildReceipt runs are not complete, so we report no comparison on it.

\subsection{Ablation study}
\label{sec:ablations}
\paragraph{Ablation study for the two components.} On the runs of \cref{tab:ladder} (supplementary \cref{tab:ablation}) and on every seed, the memory alone lifts full-label F1 over naive training by $\nAblMemMin$ to $\nAblMemMax$ points, and the blocked readout alone by \nAblReadMin{} to \nAblReadMax; together by $\nAblBothMin$ to $\nAblBothMax$, more than the two add up to. Per-session deltas instead are above CoLaR-SBR under both metrics.

\paragraph{Ablation study for the memory format.} The memory format does not change the finding (three seeds, CIL-CORD): raw documents under the same readout, with the whole network trainable where CoLaR freezes the embeddings and layers $1..4$, reach $\vErSbrFull$ full-label F1 against $\vSbrFull$, seed-matched differences (raw minus latent) of \nErSbrDiffFullMin{} to \nErSbrDiffFullMax{} points (\nErSbrDiffAaMin{} to \nErSbrDiffAaMax{} in AA).

\paragraph{Ablation study for the background logit.} We read the shared normalisation (H3) as the channel through which stale labels act, as negatives for every class; then separation should work with a fixed background. With background logits at zero, as in ICE-O, memory and blocked readout stay above CoLaR and below joint training on every seed under both metrics, recover most but not all of CoLaR-SBR's gain over CoLaR on seeds 42 and 7, and are above CoLaR-SBR on seed 123: the reading is partly supported; separation does most of the work, and the learned background adds on two of three seeds.

\paragraph{Ablation study for the memory (H4).} On CIL-CORD SBR-Recall reaches $\vRecallFull$ full-label F1 ($\vRecall$ AA) with no stored document, within $\nRecallJointGap$ points of joint training on every seed under both metrics, at the price of one inference pass per session (five on CIL-CORD; \cref{app:method}). Our port of ICE-O (frozen encoder, one head per session over a constant Other logit, no memory) forgets nothing (final row within $\nIceODevMax$ points of its diagonal on every seed) and reaches $\vIceOFull$ full-label F1 ($\vIceO$ AA), below SBR-Recall on every seed under both metrics; under our $10$-epoch cap sessions 4 and 5 were still improving and session~3 early-stopped at $\nIceOThirdMax$ F1 on every seed, so this is a lower bound for ICE-O. On Mixed without a memory, SBR-Recall scores $\nRecallMixNoMem$ AA$^\dagger$ (seed 42): as predicted, the blocks of the other schemas fall silent until their domain returns, and with the $50$-document memory restored it scores $\nRecallMixMem$ (\cref{app:results}).

\label{sec:cost}

CoLaR stores $\nMemColarKB$\,KB per document ($\nMemColarMB$\,MB for the $250$ documents of CIL-CORD; \cref{fig:memory}), $\nMemLatentOverColar\times$ less than the fp16 layer-$4$ activations it compresses. Raw replay keeps $\nMemRawKB$\,KB per document as our code holds it, with fp32 pixels and int64 ids and boxes ($\nMemRawMB$\,MB), and would take $\nMemRawEightMB$\,MB with 8-bit pixels. SBR-Recall keeps one delta per session instead: $\nMemRecallFpSessMB$\,MB in fp32 as trained ($\nMemRecallFpMB$\,MB in all), or $\nMemRecallSessMB$\,MB ($\nMemRecallMB$\,MB) at two bytes per parameter, a reporting choice that no run evaluated.

Above a compression floor, bytes do not separate the methods; the readout does: every store we ran at full rank (rank-$128$ latent or raw documents, or deltas) ends within $\nSbrGapFullMax$ full-label points of joint training on every seed through the learned-background blocked readout, and the same latent bytes under a shared softmax stay at $\vColarFull$. At rank $128$ CoLaR saves no bytes: it is $\nMemColarOverRawEight\times$ larger than raw documents with 8-bit pixels, and $\nMemRawOverColar\times$ smaller only against raw documents as our code holds them. What it gives instead is a whole-document memory without pixels or token ids; latent replay needs the frozen lower layers, so it trains $\nMemTrainSbrPct\%$ of the parameters our raw run trains (a raw memory could freeze the same layers), with full-label F1 within $\nErSbrAbsFullMax$ points of raw replay on every seed (\cref{sec:ablations}). The rank is the one knob that sets its size, but at rank $32$ ($\nMemColarThirtyTwoMB$\,MB) CoLaR-SBR falls to $\vSbrRankThirtyTwoFull$ full-label F1, $\nSbrRankThirtyTwoDropMin$ to $\nSbrRankThirtyTwoDropMax$ points below rank $128$ and below plain CoLaR in full-label F1 on every seed; rank $64$ is not evaluated. SBR-Recall's cost grows with the sessions, not their documents: it is cheaper only when a session's documents take more than its delta.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\paragraph{Limitations.} We cover five document datasets and four encoder-only backbones; the three datasets with full controls (FUNSD, CORD, SROIE) are English, with test sets of $50$ to $347$ documents; the ladder's controls ran on LayoutLMv3 and one class-incremental partition of CORD, several also on CIL-FUNSD ($50$ test forms; \cref{sec:beyond}), where the current side is needed and slicing it alone falls short; why the streams differ is untested, and one FUNSD joint seed stopped at its first epoch. Every CIL-CORD control rests on three training seeds and one test set of $\nCordTestDocs$ CORD receipts: ``on every seed'' states the same sign on all three, not significance; differences under about two points (raw against latent memory, the constant background against the learned one on seed 123, the oracle above CoLaR-SBR) are not resolved, and the replay ratio (four stored per current batch of two), which may set the size of the suppression, was not varied. SBR-Recall on Mixed, the design-choice ablations and the image probe are single- or few-seed diagnostics. We did not run several comparators (among them PLOP, BPF, MiB's distillation and per-class sigmoid heads; \cref{sec:h2}). Joint training is a reference, not a bound, and ICE-O ran under our recipe, still improving at the epoch cap. SBR-Recall grows by a delta and a pass per session and freezes its base after session~1, so it may depend on session order. CoLaR's memory grows with the sessions; at rank $128$ it takes more bytes than raw documents with 8-bit pixels, and at rank $32$ it loses most of its accuracy; storing no raw pixels or token ids is weaker than privacy.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} We investigated why replay fails in continual KIE under label growth and proposed CoLaR-SBR. On CIL-CORD, CoLaR-SBR achieves $\nSbrFull$ full-label F1, against $\nJointFull$ for joint training and $\nColarFull$ for the same memory with a shared softmax. Two things go wrong, and a memory repairs only drift (on CIL-CORD and DIL); the background conflict is removed mostly by a per-session readout on both streams: it lets an ``O'' label count only against the classes its annotator could see. On CIL-CORD true stored labels remove it almost entirely (an oracle, within $\nOraclePreJointGap$ points of joint training on every seed), a MiB-style loss about half, while a softmax slice does not; on CIL-FUNSD a MiB-style loss closes most of it and true stored labels do not. On one schema, with label growth as routing key, per-session deltas replace the memory.\label{mainend}% last line of the main text (make_submission.sh checks its page)
