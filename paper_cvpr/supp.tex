\section{Setup details}
\label{app:setup}

\paragraph{DIL unified schema.} A single four-entity label space \{HEADER, KEY, VALUE, OTHER\} (9 BIO tags) is held fixed over FUNSD$\rightarrow$SROIE$\rightarrow$CORD. FUNSD: HEADER$\rightarrow$HEADER, QUESTION$\rightarrow$KEY, ANSWER$\rightarrow$VALUE. SROIE: COMPANY$\rightarrow$KEY; ADDRESS/DATE/TOTAL$\rightarrow$VALUE. CORD: menu/sub\_total/total$\rightarrow$VALUE; void\_menu/sub$\rightarrow$OTHER. KEY originates only from FUNSD and SROIE, so the schema is VALUE-dominant; we report per-class F1 for every DIL result and treat a high aggregate as retention only if the sparse classes are retained. The reverse order is used as a robustness check.

\paragraph{CIL-CORD sessions.} CORD's 30 fine-grained classes in five sessions of six, ordered easy-to-hard by super-class: (0) core menu fields; (1) extended menu + sub; (2) sub\_total fields; (3) total / payment methods; (4) residual total + void/auxiliary. Every receipt containing at least one in-session entity is kept, with out-of-session tokens relabelled to ``O'', giving per-session training pools of 800/382/549/723/780 receipts. Class imbalance across sessions is deliberately left unmodified. Joint uses the full-label pool (each receipt once with all its labels) rather than the concatenation of the masked per-session copies, which would feed conflicting labels for the same document.

\paragraph{Mixed.} Six sessions: CORD-menu $\rightarrow$ FUNSD $\rightarrow$ CORD-menu-extended $\rightarrow$ SROIE $\rightarrow$ CORD-sub\_total $\rightarrow$ FUNSD-revisit; boundaries alternate new-class and new-domain transitions and the final session is an explicit retention check.

\paragraph{Datasets.} FUNSD (\texttt{nielsr/funsd-layoutlmv3}; 149/50), CORD (\texttt{naver-clova-ix/cord-v2}; 800/100/100), SROIE (\texttt{mp-02/sroie}; 626/347). Dataset-provided OCR and boxes throughout; images 224$\times$224; sequence length 512.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters.} Every run uses AdamW, lr $5\times10^{-5}$ constant, weight decay $0.01$ and clipping $1.0$. EWC $\lambda{=}10^3$, Fisher over 200 documents; LwF $T{=}2$, $\alpha{=}1$; ER/DER++ reservoir of 200 samples, DER++ $\alpha{=}\beta{=}0.5$. Our O-LoRA and CL-LoRA ports train a single rank-$8$ LoRA adapter on the query, key and value projections of the frozen pre-trained encoder, shared by all sessions and retrained in each with an orthogonality penalty ($\lambda{=}0.5$) against its own earlier states; CL-LoRA adds distillation towards the previous model ($T{=}2$). The published methods keep one adapter per task, so these rows report our ports, not the published designs. Early stopping uses patience 2 and $\delta{=}0.1$ F1 points. Seeds 42/7/123. The epoch cap, numeric precision, early-stopping split and memory size differ between groups of legacy-protocol ($\dagger$) cells; \cref{tab:legacyprotocol} lists them, and the clean protocol is specified in Sec.~3 of the main paper. Image CIL (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R; ViT-B/16, 10 sessions): every reported cell, including the frozen-backbone and replay baselines, was trained on a 6\,GB GPU at batch size 16 in fp32 with gradient checkpointing, 100-epoch cap with early stopping (patience 2), seeds 42/7/123; the new CODA-Prompt, CL-LoRA and LCA image cells use this recipe unchanged.

\paragraph{Compute.} Every run fits a single GPU; the full generality grid is roughly 200 GPU-hours on a 24\,GB card.

\paragraph{Ridge decomposition of the plastic-head gap.} \cref{tab:probe} reports a ridge-readout probe on the trained trunk of our replay rows (seed 42). After the last session the trunk is frozen and a closed-form ridge readout (random projection to $10{,}000$ ReLU features, $\lambda{=}10$, the RanPAC recipe) is fitted under three data conditions: on the stored memory only (deployable), on all training data of all sessions (a non-deployable ceiling on what the features support), and on all data through the frozen pre-trained trunk (image only). Three differences follow: the \emph{head gap} (full ridge minus the trained head) is the total headroom of the readout; the \emph{coverage gap} (full minus stored) is the part that needs features of inputs the memory does not hold; the \emph{trunk gap} (adapted minus frozen) is what replay-driven adaptation bought. On images the head gap is $9.5/16.1$ points and is mostly coverage ($7.5/14.3$), so a better analytic head on the stored latents does not close the gap to SLCA; the adapted trunk is nevertheless $2.9/15.0$ points better than the frozen one under the same readout, so adaptation is not the problem. On documents the head gap of CoLaR is within one point on DIL and Mixed and $7.7$ points on CIL-CORD with zero coverage gap, i.e.\ the compressed store already covers its documents and the residual is the plastic head itself. The probe reads every past training image once at the end and is therefore a diagnostic, not a continual method.

\paragraph{Null cells among the fixed baselines (CPFD, IS3, CODA-Prompt).} Three of the fixed baselines do not converge on the document scenarios, and we report those cells as nulls rather than as scores. CPFD and IS3 were ported onto the single growing token-classification head used by every other method here (the reference implementations keep per-task classifiers). On CIL-CORD, CPFD learns the first session ($90$--$95$ F1) and then reads $0.00$ on every session from the first class-adding boundary (AA $2.7$ and $0.0$ on seeds 42 and 123); on Mixed it reads $6.0$, $0.0$ and $0.1$; and on DIL, where no class is ever added, the first domain falls to $8.0/4.0/0.9$ F1 at the first boundary and the final row is zero on every domain for seed 42 and $3.0/9.7/0.0$ for seed 7 (AA $0.0/4.2/14.2$, reported as $6.1\pm7.3$ since one seed does learn the last domain), so the collapse is not a property of head growth. Two single-knob ablations on seed 42 reproduce the collapse exactly: flooring the per-sample CE weight at $0.5$ (session-1 F1 $0.00$) and switching the distillation term off entirely (session-1 F1 $2.1$), so neither the adaptive CE factor nor the attention/logit distillation is the cause; what remains is the pseudo-labelling and O-token masking path, which removes the training signal on the new session's entities. IS3 learns each new session (diagonal $93/29/32/66/10$ on seed 123) but never predicts a previously learned class again (every off-diagonal entry is exactly $0.00$), giving AA $4.4\pm2.2$ on CIL-CORD; on Mixed, whose last session adds no classes, it recovers to $30.1\pm1.2$ and that cell is reported. On DIL the nine-tag head never grows, so IS3's old/new split is degenerate (every entity row is ``old'' at every boundary and the O-token distillation spans the whole output) and the method reduces to sequential fine-tuning under a full-output distillation term; seed 42 learns the first domain ($88.2$) and drops both earlier domains below $8$ F1 at the next boundary (AA $38.3$ vs.\ naive $41.3$). We report that single seed rather than spend further compute on a structural limitation of the port. CODA-Prompt reads AA $0.0$ on all three CIL-CORD seeds with its default pool of $10$ components; enlarging the pool to $50$ ($10$ per session) lets some sessions be learned (diagonal $79.5/40.6/0.0/18.9/36.8$) but the final row is $[0,0,0,0,36.8]$, AA $7.4$, with one session never learned, so the cell stays a null. These are properties of the methods' O-tag and static-pool assumptions under five class-adding sessions, not of the sessions themselves: the same sessions are learned by naive training (AA $19.0$) and by joint training ($33.3$), both under legacy scoring.

\paragraph{Routing which stored document to replay (null).} The OCR vocabulary of a document is set by the input and does not drift with training, which suggests a drift-free sampler: a softmax over term-frequency signature scores $s_j=\sigma(x)^{\top}\sigma_j$ in three directions (\emph{near}, \emph{far}, \emph{task}), uniform as the zero-point. The seed-42 reading was promising (far $+1.8$ over uniform $86.4$ at $d{=}50$), so we fixed that single arm in advance for confirmation. Across three seeds the gap collapses to $+1.8/+0.4/-0.6$, mean $+0.5$ pp with a sign flip, inside the uniform-sampling spread: lexical routing is a null, and the CoLaR results do not depend on it.

\paragraph{Rebalancing and reading the memory (nulls).} On DIL with LayoutLMv3 (CoLaR, $k{=}4$, $r{=}128$, $50$ documents per task; base $87.6$ on seed 42), three changes to how the memory is filled or weighted leave AA where it was: a class-balanced replay loss gives $87.1$, coverage-based ($k$-centre) document selection $86.8$ and entity-weighted compression $87.1$ (seed 42 each). Each trades FUNSD retention for SROIE retention at about $1{:}1$, so the changes redistribute retention between tasks rather than add to it. A three-seed check finds no lever above the base (class-balanced $87.8$, $k$-centre $87.0$, against $\vColarDil$ for the base). Two read-side additions are negative: blending a $k$NN readout over the stored latents into the head is inert or harmful, and metaplastic consolidation of the readout lowers AA from $85.9$ to $79.5$ at its own operating point.

\paragraph{A bounded positive on the storage side.} Replaying pseudo-labelled \emph{public} documents through the same mechanism (zero private bytes) recovers dense-label tasks (FUNSD $76.1$) but not one the proxy pool does not cover (SROIE $\sim7$), topping out at $58.9$: coverage-limited transfer. Budget-adaptive allocation across tasks moves along a Pareto frontier, not beyond it (\cref{app:extra}).


\section{Drift analysis details and legacy-scored tables}
\label{sec:supp-drift}
\paragraph{Displacement by depth, with and without the head.} For a checkpoint pair $(\theta^{(t-1)},\theta^{(t)})$ and a parameter group $g$ we compute the old-task-Fisher-weighted displacement $D_g=\sum_{\phi\in g}F^{(t-1)}_\phi(\theta^{(t)}_\phi-\theta^{(t-1)}_\phi)^2$. \cref{tab:depthprofile} reports it at the first task boundary, mean over the live seeds of each condition. The classifier's $D_g$ is two to three orders of magnitude above the largest encoder bucket on every condition ($111\times$ to $1608\times$). This ratio is inflated by construction: the empirical Fisher of a token classifier is largest at the head whatever the task ($0.22$ against $0.005$ for the next group on LayoutLMv3), so the weighting itself favours the head. The informative part is the encoder-only share: with the head excluded, weighted displacement sits in the early and middle layers on every document encoder and is smallest in the late layers. Representational drift measured by CKA, in contrast, grows with depth because it accumulates. The two instruments therefore agree on the classifier and disagree on the late encoder layers, and the main text claims only what both support.

\paragraph{What the significance tests do and do not show.} Two tests accompanied an earlier version of this analysis and we report them with their limits. (i) A Mann--Whitney test of the classifier against each other parameter group (six samples per group, Bonferroni $\alpha'=0.0042$) rejects equality against three of twelve groups ($p=0.0022$) and not against the rest; it was run on per-group Fisher \emph{importance}, which is head-dominant by construction, and we do not use it as evidence of where forgetting occurs. (ii) A permutation test on the normalised $D_g$ depth profile does not reject a shared profile between LayoutLMv3 and LiLT or BROS ($p=0.38$, $0.51$); since every normalised profile is nearly one-hot at the head, this test cannot reject and is not evidence of architecture-generality. The claim that drift is shared across backbones rests on the descriptive agreement of the CKA profiles and of \cref{tab:depthprofile}, and on the remedy ordering of \cref{tab:grid}.

\paragraph{Consolidation probe.} A depth-targeted consolidation method (output distillation with a per-bucket penalty) scores $42.6$ AA on DIL when the penalty is spread uniformly over all depth buckets, $84.7$ when concentrated on the head and late layers, $86.1$ head-only and $86.2$ late-only (three seeds). All four arms include a $200$-document head-replay buffer, and plain ER with the same buffer scores $87.9$. The probe therefore shows that a uniform penalty prevents the new tasks from being learned; it does not show that a targeted penalty recovers old tasks without replay, and we do not cite it for that.
